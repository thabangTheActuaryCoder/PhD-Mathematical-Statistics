## 1. Verdict

**MAJOR.**

Every part of the chapter that claims to be proved was re-derived line by line and it survives: the sharpness theorem is correct with constant exactly one, the far-mass mixture genuinely gives only $\varepsilon C_T(P)\approx\varepsilon(1-\alpha)$, the exact minimiser $b^\*=(2(r+1))^{2/(2r+3)}N^{3/(2r+3)}$ and every entry of its table are right (23.935 at $r=2$, $N=500$, against the non-minimising balance point 14.345, derivative verified to vanish at $b^\*$), and the whole of the "blocking is rate-suboptimal" argument — covariance $\le\alpha(k)\le\beta(k)$, $\Var F_N\le(\tfrac14+2C\zeta(r))/N$, the two-sided Chebyshev step at $q_{u\mp\varepsilon}$, and the Le Cam two-point bound with $\gamma=(1-4\delta)\sqrt{2/N}$, $c=\gamma\alpha/3$ — is correct as written. The verdict is nevertheless MAJOR and not MINOR for four reasons that touch the chapter's headline statements rather than its details: the confidence range of Theorem~\ref{thm:block-lower} contradicts the range its own proof route can reach; the practical conclusion that blocking wins at every record length a bank possesses is an artefact of using Chebyshev for the unblocked route while the blocked route is given its exact Beta law, and reverses under the Fuk–Nagaev bound the chapter itself invokes forty lines later; $b^\*$ is presented as "the optimal block length" when it is the minimiser of an upper bound with two constants set to one, with no lower bound for the blocked route anywhere; and the two routes are compared on unequal terms, the blocked bound carrying the deployment-side coupling inside $(n+1)\beta(b)$ and the unblocked bound carrying none. Add one sentence narrating the thesis's own earlier position, one duplicated definition, three verbatim overlaps with Chapters 2 and 3, a 25-line chapter abstract against a 5–8 line rule, and one wrong integer that has been corrected directly, and the chapter needs a revision pass rather than a polish.

### 2. [FIX] The "blocked wins at every record length" conclusion is an artefact of Chebyshev — must
**Location.** §4.5.2, Remark~\ref{rem:sharp-constants} (table and the sentence beginning "The honest summary is that blocking is rate-suboptimal and constant-optimal in the range of interest"); §4.10.2 design rule, step 4; §4.11 "What blocking buys, stated once", last two sentences.
**What to change.** The blocked column of the table is the *exact* bound $g(b)=1/(2\sqrt{n+2})+(n+1)Cb^{-r}$, resting on the exact Beta law. The unblocked column is Chebyshev, whose $\delta$-dependence is $\delta^{-1/2}=4.47$ at $\delta=0.05$. Comparing the two and concluding that blocking is "constant-optimal in the range of interest" compares the sharpest available inequality against the bluntest. Section~4.6.3 already names the right tool: the Fuk–Nagaev inequality for bounded strongly mixing sequences, whose Gaussian-type term at $\delta=0.05$ is of order $\sqrt{2\sigma^2\log(2/\delta)/N}$ with $\sigma^2=\tfrac14+2C\zeta(r)=3.79$, that is $5.3N^{-1/2}$, while its polynomial term at $N=10^4,r=2,\delta=0.05$ is $(N^r\delta)^{-1/(r+1)}=0.0059$ and does not bind. With $5.3N^{-1/2}$ in place of $11.90N^{-1/2}$ the crossover moves from $N\approx10^5$ to $N\approx2.5\times10^3$, about ten years of daily data, and the unblocked route already wins at $N=10^4$ (0.053 against the blocked 0.072). Either (a) add a third row to the table headed "unblocked, sub-Gaussian estimate $5.3N^{-1/2}$" with a `\datanote{}` marking it an estimate and not a proved bound, and rewrite the closing sentence to say that the blocked route wins only against the proved Chebyshev bound and that a Bernstein-type bound for mixing sequences would move the crossover to a few thousand periods; or (b) delete the "constant-optimal in the range of interest" claim and step 4's "Below about $10^5$ periods at $r=2$ the blocked bound wins" and replace both with "the comparison is between an exact bound and a Chebyshev bound and is therefore not a comparison of the two routes". Item 15 below supplies the locator that (a) needs.
**Why.** This is the chapter's only practical recommendation and it is carried into Chapters 6 and 7 by the "Connection to the empirical chapters" paragraph. As written the recommendation is an artefact of an inequality choice, and the chapter has the better inequality in hand two sections later.
**Priority.** must.

### 4. [FIX] The blocked and unblocked routes are compared on unequal terms — must
**Location.** §4.5.2 Theorem~\ref{thm:block-rate}(c) and Remark~\ref{rem:sharp-constants}; §4.10.2 step 4; §4.3 paragraph "Why the drift and the deployment gap are excluded".
**What to change.** $g(b)=1/(2\sqrt{n+2})+(n+1)\beta(b)$ charges $n+1$ couplings, of which one is the deployment block (Theorem~\ref{thm:block-b}(a) says so explicitly: "adding the coupling of the deployment block ... gives the stated $(n+1)\beta(b)$"). A bank cannot use the unblocked route without paying that decoupling: a gap of length $b_g$ before the deployment block and a Markov term $\beta(b_g)/\delta'$, which at $C=1$, $r=2$, $b_g=10$, $\delta'=0.05$ is $0.2$ — larger than either column of the table at $N\ge10^4$. **Why.** The comparison decides which route the empirical chapters report, and as written it credits the unblocked route with a decoupling it has not paid for while debiting the blocked route for one it has.
**Priority.** must.
