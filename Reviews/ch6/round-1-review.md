|---|---|---|---|---|
| n = ⌊250/(2b)⌋ | 10 | **9** | **8** | 8 |
| k = ⌈0.9(n+1)⌉ | 10 | **9** | **9** | 9 |
| k ≤ n ? | yes | **yes** | **no** | no |
| k/(n+1) | 0.9091 | **0.9000** | — | — |

So b = 13 is the last feasible block length and b = 14 gives an infinite threshold. The ceiling is robust to the accounting convention: if the leading gap is taken inside the record (`n = ⌊(N−b)/(2b)⌋`, the convention of the ch3 code lesson of 2026-09-09), the ceiling is still 13 (b ≤ 250/19 = 13.16).

## 1. Verdict

**MAJOR.**

The design is the best thing in the thesis so far: the protocol genuinely hides what has to be hidden, the windows are fixed from the cessation calendar and not from the data, the "no theorem ranks these methods" disclosure is complete and repeated where it is needed, and the equal-footing rule of Chapter 4 is applied to the route comparison more carefully than Chapter 4 applies it to itself. ## 4. Missing for a strong doctoral chapter

The chapter designs an experiment beautifully and never shows the reader what its own design is worth. Three absences do most of the damage. First, there is no **power or sizing statement anywhere**: with `M` of order twelve to twenty-five deployment blocks the one-sided binomial test of C1 has almost no power against any alternative inside the chapter's own bounds, and with weekly Wednesday marks and a strict arm that may be six weeks long, `M_P` for criterion V1 may be of order thirty to sixty strongly dependent pairs, against which a 90 % containment threshold is a coin toss. A single table — for each criterion, the number of independent-equivalent observations after the block-bootstrap correction, and the smallest departure the criterion could detect — would convert the chapter from a well-designed experiment into a well-designed and *honestly sized* one, and it is the one thing an external examiner in statistics will ask for. Second, the chapter never states the **null model** against which containment is judged: a containment rate needs a benchmark rate, and the natural one — the containment rate an interval of the same width centred on the naive mark would achieve — is computable with no extra data and would turn criterion V1 from an absolute threshold into a comparison. Third, there is no **pre-committed order of operations**: the chapter fixes thresholds in advance but not the sequence in which the arms, the grid cells and the expiries are examined, which is the remaining degree of freedom once thresholds are fixed; one paragraph pre-registering that the strict arm is examined before the exchange arm, the primary cell before the grid, and the pooled row before the per-expiry rows, would close it. Beyond these, a short subsection on what the chapter would do if the terminal export is refused — the appendix says this is a live possibility and calls it "the one refusal that would stop the first half of Chapter 6" — would show the examiner that the thesis has a plan and not only a hope.

## 6. Direct edits made to the chapter

Two, both outright errors that would mislead the next reader; neither is a style change.

1. §6.4.2, the Christoffersen independence statistic: `\hat\pi:=(n_{01}+n_{11})/M` corrected to `\hat\pi:=(n_{01}+n_{11})/(n_{00}+n_{01}+n_{10}+n_{11})`. With `M` breach indicators there are `M−1` transitions, so the pooled transition probability is not a fraction of `M`; as printed the statistic is not the likelihood-ratio statistic it is presented as.
2. §6.4.1: "`$n'=\lfloor N/b\rfloor$`, twice the certificate's `$n$`" corrected to "about twice". At the chapter's own primary block length b = 10 and N = 250, `n' = 25` and `n = 12`, so 25 ≠ 2 × 12; exact equality holds only when `2b | N`.
