\subsection{Purpose and scope}\label{appC:usd-purpose}

Chapter~\ref{ch6-usd-validation} treats the USD LIBOR-to-SOFR transition as the one benchmark transition in which the successor smile became observable. Its two tests need, on every business day of the window, (i) the observed USD LIBOR caplet and swaption smiles, (ii) the observed SOFR caplet and swaption smiles, (iii) the LIBOR--SOFR basis, (iv) the ISDA fallback spread $s$ by tenor, (v) SOFR discounting and forward curves, and (vi) the calendar of cessation events that fixes the deployment window. This section specifies each dataset: what it is, the vendor field, the public alternative, the date range, frequency, licence, and the quality issues known before any data is pulled. The section is a specification, not a report of data held. Nothing below has been downloaded yet; Section~\ref{appC:usd-minimum} states the smallest set on which Chapter~\ref{ch6-usd-validation} can run.

Notation for tenors follows the ISDA convention: O/N, 1W, 1M, 2M, 3M, 6M, 12M. Bloomberg tickers are written as \texttt{TICKER <Index>} or \texttt{TICKER <Curncy>} with the yellow-key suffix Bloomberg uses; Refinitiv identifiers are written as RICs.

Two consequences for Chapter~\ref{ch6-usd-validation}. First, the ``truth'' against which the predicted SOFR smile is compared exists as a quoted market only from roughly November~2021; before that the SOFR non-linear market is thin (Section~\ref{appC:usd-sofr-vol}). Second, the LIBOR smile after 31~December~2021 is a market in a benchmark that regulators had told banks to stop using, and after 30~June~2023 it is a synthetic rate with no option market at all. The natural fitting window is therefore 2019--2021 on LIBOR and the natural deployment window is November~2021 to June~2023 on SOFR, with 2019 chosen so that the September~2019 repo spike is inside $\Dfit$ rather than a surprise in deployment.

The 3M value, $s = 26.161$~bp, is the one Chapter~\ref{ch6-usd-validation} uses, because the USD caplet and swaption markets are written on 3M LIBOR. The fact sheet also gives the fallback-rate ticker \texttt{FUS0003M <Index>} for the all-in 3M fallback (adjusted SOFR plus spread) and confirms the underlying fixings \texttt{US0003M <Index>} (3M USD LIBOR) and \texttt{SOFRRATE <Index>} (SOFR). The \texttt{Y}-tickers are constant after 5~March~2021 and are useful only as a check; the \texttt{X}-tickers give the compounded-in-arrears SOFR over each LIBOR tenor with the two-day backward shift of the BISL Rule Book, which is exactly the realised compounded rate $R(T,T+\Delta)$ of Chapter~\ref{ch:prelim} up to the day-count and shift conventions stated there.

\datanote{The 1W and 2M spread adjustments were fixed on the same date but are not in Regulation~ZZ (the LIBOR Act covers only O/N, 1M, 3M, 6M, 12M) and the BISL technical notice of 5~March~2021 that lists all seven could not be read as text on 2026-09-09 (the PDF at \url{https://assets.bbhub.io/professional/sites/10/IBOR_Fallbacks_LIBOR_Cessation_Announcement-2021-Mar.pdf} rendered as images only). Read them from \texttt{YUS0001W} and \texttt{YUS0002M} on the terminal. Neither tenor is used in the thesis.}

\paragraph{Public alternatives.} The five LIBOR Act values are in Regulation~ZZ, 12~CFR~253.4(c), which is public. ISDA's Fallbacks FAQ (v13, August~2024) and Bloomberg's fact sheet give the methodology. ARRC's recommended spread adjustments for cash products equal the ISDA values by construction.

\paragraph{Licence.} BISL states that a licence is required for ``re-distribution or usage'' of the adjusted RFRs, spread adjustments and fallback rates, with usage-licence fees waived for institutions below USD~5bn total assets using a single rate set. Quoting the five public values and using them as the constant $s$ in an academic thesis is use of Regulation~ZZ, not of Bloomberg's data. Downloading the daily \texttt{X}-ticker history from a university terminal falls under the terminal agreement, which permits internal academic use; redistributing it in the reproducibility package does not. The package will therefore recompute the compounded SOFR from the New York Fed's SOFR series rather than ship the \texttt{X}-ticker history.

\subsection{SOFR and the SOFR OIS curve}\label{appC:usd-ois}

\paragraph{SOFR fixings.} Administered by the Federal Reserve Bank of New York; published each business day at approximately 08:00~ET with a same-day revision at about 14:30~ET if the correction exceeds one basis point; first published 3~April~2018 for value date 2~April~2018. Fields published daily: rate, 1st, 25th, 75th and 99th percentiles, and volume. The 30-, 90- and 180-day compounded averages and the SOFR Index (initial value 1.00000000 on 2~April~2018) are official from 2~March~2020 with indicative history back to April~2018.

\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item Public source: New York Fed Markets Data API, \url{https://markets.newyorkfed.org/} (endpoints under \texttt{/api/rates/secured/sofr/}, CSV, JSON and XML). Licence: the New York Fed grants ``a non-exclusive license \ldots\ to use, copy, and distribute Content'' with an attribution notice; redistribution in the reproducibility package is permitted with that notice.
\item Public mirror: FRED series \texttt{SOFR}, \texttt{SOFR30DAYAVG}, \texttt{SOFR90DAYAVG}, \texttt{SOFR180DAYAVG}, \texttt{SOFRINDEX}, \texttt{SOFR75}, \texttt{SOFRVOL} (daily, percent, not seasonally adjusted; \texttt{SOFRINDEX} from 2018-04-02).
\item Bloomberg: \texttt{SOFRRATE <Index>}. Refinitiv: \texttt{USDSOFR=} (reported on the LSEG developer forum; to be confirmed in Workspace).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{USD LIBOR fixings.} \texttt{US0003M <Index>} on Bloomberg; \texttt{USD3MFSR=} on Refinitiv (reported on the Refinitiv developer forum as the 3M USD LIBOR fixing RIC; to be confirmed). Public history is a problem: FRED removed all IBA series (including \texttt{USD3MTD156N}) on 31~January~2022 at IBA's request. IBA's own Historical LIBOR Rates webpage provides the previous day's rates ``for Your information purposes only'' and its user agreement forbids copying, redistribution and ``pricing or valuation purposes''; IBA's fee schedule prices an Educational Usage Licence at USD~5{,}000 per benchmark per year. The thesis will therefore take LIBOR fixings from the university terminal and will not ship them; the package ships code that reads a user-supplied CSV.

\paragraph{SOFR OIS curve.} Chapter~\ref{ch6-usd-validation} needs the SOFR discount curve and the forward compounded rates $F_t$ at each caplet start. Inputs: SOFR OIS par rates for 1W to 50Y (annual fixed versus compounded SOFR, Act/360, T+2) and, at the short end, SOFR futures.

\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item Bloomberg: the SOFR OIS par-rate family whose 10Y member is reported as \texttt{USOSFR10 <Curncy>} (seen in third-party material, not in a Bloomberg document; to be confirmed on terminal via \texttt{SWDF} or \texttt{ICVS} curve~490, the Bloomberg USD SOFR curve, whose number is also to be confirmed). The Bloomberg benchmark 5Y SOFR swap rate index is \texttt{USISSO05 <Index>} (from an SEC filing that names it).
\item Refinitiv: composite SOFR OIS RIC \texttt{USDSROIS=} (real-time, streaming; reported on the LSEG developer forum) and the Tradition-contributed curve chain; zero curves are on the \texttt{RFRZERO} page. Individual tenor RICs to be confirmed in Workspace.
\item Public: CME publishes daily settlement prices for one- and three-month SOFR futures (\url{https://www.cmegroup.com/markets/interest-rates/stirs/}) for view-only use; historical settlements require a CME DataMine order and licence. There is no public daily history of SOFR OIS par rates. For the deployment window the discount curve can be replaced by the realised SOFR path itself, because the certificate scores realised hedging error, not model value; only the forward-looking $F_t$ at fitting dates needs a curve.
\end{itemize}

\datanote{No SOFR OIS par-rate history is public. If the terminal export of the OIS strip is refused or incomplete, the fallback is to bootstrap $F_t$ from CME SOFR futures settlements out to two years (the longest caplet expiry the thesis uses) and to state the convexity adjustment used. CME settlement history for 2019--2023 must then be licensed through DataMine or transcribed from the terminal (\texttt{SFRA <Cmdty>} is the front three-month SOFR future; to be confirmed).}

\subsection{USD LIBOR cap/floor and swaption volatility surfaces, 2019--2023}\label{appC:usd-libor-vol}

\paragraph{What is needed.} For each business day in $\Dfit$: (a) cap/floor normal volatilities on 3M USD LIBOR for expiries 1Y to 10Y and strikes ATM and a fixed absolute grid (at least 1\%, 1.5\%, 2\%, 2.5\%, 3\%, 4\%, 5\%, plus ATM$\pm$25, $\pm$50, $\pm$100~bp where quoted), from which caplet volatilities are stripped; (b) ATM swaption normal volatilities on the expiry~$\times$~tail grid \{1M, 3M, 6M, 1Y, 2Y, 5Y\}~$\times$~\{1Y, 2Y, 5Y, 10Y\} and, where available, the smile at $\pm$25, $\pm$50, $\pm$100~bp. Normal (basis-point) volatility is the market convention throughout the window; lognormal quotes, if downloaded, must be converted with the forward of the same day.

\paragraph{Bloomberg.} Volatility cube monitor \texttt{VCUB <GO>} (verified as Bloomberg's interest-rate volatility cube monitor) with source \texttt{BVOL} (Bloomberg's composite, derived from contributed broker quotes) or \texttt{BBIR}. Cap volatilities are stripped on \texttt{VCUB} into caplet volatilities and can be exported by strike and expiry. Swaption ATM normal volatilities are carried by the \texttt{USSN\ldots <Curncy>} family (third-party sources name \texttt{USSNAC10} and \texttt{USSNAC2}; the encoding of expiry and tail in the suffix is to be confirmed on terminal, and the smile tickers are to be confirmed). Cap volatility tickers: to be confirmed on terminal. Daily history is available through \texttt{HP} or the Desktop API (\texttt{BDH}) under the standard terminal agreement, which permits internal academic use but not redistribution.

\paragraph{Refinitiv.} Swaption and cap/floor surfaces are served through Instrument Pricing Analytics (volatility-surface endpoints, \texttt{NormalizedVolatility} output) rather than as fixed RICs; historical surfaces depend on the Workspace entitlement. RICs to be confirmed.

\paragraph{Public alternatives.} None for the surface. The CME options on three-month SOFR futures (listed from January~2020; to be confirmed) give a public smile on the short end only and are on SOFR, not LIBOR. There is no public USD LIBOR cap or swaption volatility history.

\paragraph{Frequency and range.} Daily close, 2~January~2019 to 30~June~2023 for LIBOR. After 31~December~2021 the LIBOR surface is a shrinking interdealer market and after 30~June~2023 it does not exist; Chapter~\ref{ch6-usd-validation} uses LIBOR quotes only to 31~December~2021 for fitting and reports the 2022--2023 LIBOR quotes, if downloaded, as a diagnostic of a dying market rather than as data.

\paragraph{Known quality issues.}
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
\item Composite surfaces are filled. Bloomberg's \texttt{BVOL} and Refinitiv's IPA surfaces interpolate and extrapolate where brokers did not quote; off-ATM swaption quotes between August~2019 and April~2020 are documented in the literature as largely missing, so a smile downloaded for that period may be model output. The thesis records, for each day and point, whether the vendor flags the point as quoted or derived, where the vendor exposes that flag; where it does not, the thesis treats strikes beyond $\pm$100~bp as unquoted.
\item Negative-rate handling. In 2020--2021 the ATM 3M forward fell to about 0.2\%; quotes in the shifted-lognormal convention may appear alongside normal quotes. The convention field must be exported with each value.
\item Strike grid drift. Absolute strike grids are re-centred by vendors as rates move; a strike present in 2019 may be absent in 2021. The stripping code in Appendix~\ref{appC-data-code} interpolates in strike only within the quoted range.
\item The 2022 hiking cycle. Front-end LIBOR volatility in 2022 is in the SOFR-first regime with LIBOR quotes derived from SOFR quotes plus the fixed spread. Fitting on LIBOR in 2022 would fit a transformed SOFR surface and would not test the thesis.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{SOFR cap/floor and swaption volatility surfaces, emergence to 2024}\label{appC:usd-sofr-vol}

\paragraph{What is needed.} The same grids as Section~\ref{appC:usd-libor-vol} on compounded 3M SOFR (caps) and on SOFR OIS swaps (swaptions), daily, from the first date the vendor shows a quoted (not derived) SOFR surface to 31~December~2024. The early part of this history is the observed successor smile that Chapter~\ref{ch6-usd-validation} compares with the smile predicted from LIBOR under the identifiability theorem of Chapter~\ref{ch5-identifiability} plus the $\pm\eta$ interval; the later part is the deployment sample for the certificate.

\paragraph{Emergence dates.} Clarus SDR data show SOFR swaptions from May~2021 and the first day above USD~2bn notional on 8~November~2021 (13\% of USD swaption notional that day; 29\% in the following week; USD~32bn against USD~80bn of LIBOR swaptions). SOFR caps and floors were trading before that date, at 19\% of cap/floor notional in the week of 8--12~November~2021. Risk.net reported in September~2022 that SOFR swaption volatility data ``from major vendors remains unavailable'' and that banks were mapping LIBOR swaption surfaces to SOFR by shifting strikes by the spread. The date on which Bloomberg's \texttt{BVOL} SOFR surface switched from derived to quoted is therefore the first thing to establish on the terminal.

\paragraph{Bloomberg.} \texttt{VCUB <GO>} with the SOFR underlying selected; Bloomberg states that it builds RFR volatility cubes and prices SOFR caps, floors and swaptions from them. SOFR cap normal volatilities are quoted in basis points on a tenor~$\times$~strike table (the Deriscope SOFR cap example of 30~December~2022 shows the layout). Tickers for SOFR cap and swaption volatilities: to be confirmed on terminal. Settlement convention matters for swaptions: interdealer SOFR swaptions were physically settled from November~2021 pending a tradeable SOFR swap-rate benchmark; ISDA Supplement~88 (10~November~2021) and Settlement Matrix v3.0 set the cash-settlement rate for USD SOFR transactions to the USD SOFR ICE Swap Rate, with Collateralised Cash Price the USD standard. Export the settlement type with each quote.

\paragraph{Refinitiv.} IPA volatility surfaces with the SOFR underlying; RICs to be confirmed.

\paragraph{Public alternatives.} CME options on three-month SOFR futures: daily settlement prices are viewable on cmegroup.com and the implied smile on the first eight quarterly contracts is a genuine public successor smile out to two years. This is the only public SOFR option market and is sufficient for the identifiability test at the 3M~$\times$~\{3M,\ldots,2Y\} expiries, though not for the swaption grid. Historical settlements need a DataMine licence.

\paragraph{Known quality issues.}
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
\item Derived versus quoted. Until vendors had SOFR broker quotes, the SOFR surface was the LIBOR surface shifted by $s$ with volatility carried across. A predicted-versus-observed comparison on such data is circular. The thesis reports the identifiability test only from the first month in which the vendor surface is marked as quoted, and states that month.
\item Compounded versus Term SOFR caps. Dealer caps trade on compounded-in-arrears SOFR; loan-market caps trade on CME Term SOFR. CME Term SOFR derivatives are restricted to end-user hedging, so the Term SOFR option market is thin and one-sided. The thesis uses compounded caps only.
\item Convention change. The 2021 ISDA Definitions took effect for cleared trades on 2--3~October~2021; quotes straddling that weekend may change day count or payment lag.
\item FOMC-date discontinuities. SOFR forwards jump at FOMC dates; the SABR-on-a-continuous-forward description of Chapter~\ref{ch:prelim} holds between meeting dates, and short-expiry SOFR caplets spanning a meeting are excluded from the smile fit \cite{brace2024}.
\item Repo spikes. SOFR printed 5.25\% on 17~September~2019 against 2.43\% the day before; the compounded 3M rate absorbs this as roughly 3~bp, but daily-frequency scores must not treat the spike as a data error. The New York Fed's revision policy (one-basis-point threshold, 14:30~ET) means the 08:00 and 14:30 prints can differ; use the final print.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{LIBOR--SOFR basis swaps}\label{appC:usd-basis}

\paragraph{What is needed.} Daily par spreads of 3M USD LIBOR versus compounded SOFR basis swaps, 1Y to 30Y, 2019 to April~2023, to estimate the basis process $B_t$ under the physical measure: its level, volatility $\eta$ and the correlations $\rho_{BF}$, $\rho_{B\alpha}$ that Chapter~\ref{ch5-identifiability} shows the option market does not identify. The historical estimate is the ambiguity set the certificate of the certification chapter takes as input, and Chapter~\ref{ch6-usd-validation} tests whether it was wide enough.

\paragraph{Refinitiv.} The Tradition-contributed curve \texttt{USDSR3LBS=TPSR} (3M LIBOR versus SOFR basis), with tenor points \texttt{USDSR3L3M=TPSR}, \texttt{USDSR3L6M=TPSR}, \texttt{USDSR3L1Y=TPSR}, \ldots, \texttt{USDSR3L50Y=TPSR} (reported on the LSEG developer forum as the RICs for the individual curve points). The ten-year point \texttt{USDSR3L10Y=} also appears without the contributor suffix. History depth to be confirmed; contributed curves often have short history in Workspace.

\paragraph{Bloomberg.} Basis-swap tickers: to be confirmed on terminal (the LIBOR/SOFR basis family is reachable from \texttt{SWPM} with the basis-swap template and from \texttt{ICVS}; the ticker roots are not given in any Bloomberg document read for this section).

\paragraph{Public alternatives.} None for quoted basis swaps. Two substitutes: (i) the realised basis, $\text{3M LIBOR fixing} - \text{compounded SOFR over the same period} - s$, computable from the fixings in Section~\ref{appC:usd-ois} and the definition of the BISL \texttt{X}-tickers; (ii) the Fed-funds--SOFR and LIBOR--Fed-funds basis pairs, whose cleared versions were converted by CME and LCH in April--May~2023 and whose history is on the same terminals. The realised basis is what the thesis's $B_t$ denotes; the quoted basis swap is its risk-neutral expectation and is needed only as a check on drift.

\paragraph{Known quality issues.} Quoted basis spreads compress mechanically toward $s$ as June~2023 approaches, because the cleared swaps were converted at the fixed spread; the last twelve months of the series are a convergence trade, not a free market. Estimation of $\eta$ uses 2019 to mid-2022 only. Realised basis from fixings is available for the whole of 2018--2023 and is the primary series.

\subsection{Minimum viable data}\label{appC:usd-minimum}

Chapter~\ref{ch6-usd-validation} can be written on three terminal exports and two public downloads. The terminal exports are: the daily 3M USD LIBOR fixing from 2~January~2018 to 30~June~2023; the daily \texttt{VCUB} caplet-volatility strip on 3M LIBOR at ATM and $\pm$50, $\pm$100~bp for expiries 3M to 2Y from 2~January~2019 to 31~December~2021; and the same strip on compounded SOFR from the first quoted month to 31~December~2024. The public downloads are the New York Fed SOFR series from 2~April~2018 and the five Regulation~ZZ spread adjustments. From these five inputs the realised compounded rate $R(T,T+\Delta)$, the realised basis $B_t$, the constant $s=26.161$~bp, the fitted SABR smiles, the predicted SOFR smile with its $\pm\eta$ interval and the realised hedging-error scores of the certificate all follow. Everything else in Table~\ref{tab:usd-register} improves the chapter: the swaption grid extends the identifiability test beyond two years, the OIS strip replaces the futures-implied forward, the quoted basis swaps check the drift of $B_t$, and the CME options give a public cross-check of the SOFR smile. If the terminal export of volatilities is refused, the chapter cannot be written on public data alone, because no public LIBOR option surface exists; the CME SOFR options then support only the second half of the chapter, and Chapter~\ref{ch1-introduction} must say so.

This section lists every South African dataset that Chapter~\ref{ch7-zar-coda} consumes, classifies each as \emph{free} (public download), \emph{terminal} (a Bloomberg or LSEG licence held by the university or a sponsor) or \emph{sponsor-only} (dealer-confidential, obtainable only under a data agreement), and records the access route, the format and the coverage. Access dates are 9~September 2026. Table~\ref{tab:appC-zar} summarises; the closing paragraph states what the chapter does if no sponsor signs.

\subsection{ZARONIA daily fixings}\label{sec:appC-zaronia}

\emph{Free.} The South African Reserve Bank (SARB) administers ZARONIA and publishes the fixing for each South African business day at 10:00, with a republication window to 12:00 for corrections. Publication began on 2~November 2022. Rates for 1~November 2022 to 3~November 2023 are labelled ``observation period rates'' and rates from 4~November 2023 ``official publication rates''; rates dated before 31~October 2022 are labelled ``pre-publication or proxy rates''. All three classes are served from the same page,
\begin{quote}\footnotesize\raggedright\url{https://www.resbank.co.za/en/home/what-we-do/financial-markets/south-african-overnight-index-average}
\end{quote}
through a date-range selector that exports CSV, XML or PDF; there is no static file URL, so the reproducibility protocol records the selected date range and the download timestamp with the exported CSV. The SARB also publishes compounded ZARONIA period averages (1~week, 1, 3, 6, 9 and 12 months) and a compounded index with base $100$ on 1~November 2022, in the same three formats, at
\begin{quote}\footnotesize\raggedright\url{https://www.resbank.co.za/en/home/what-we-do/financial-markets/compounded-zaronia-period-averages-and-index}
\end{quote}
The thesis computes $R(T,T+\Delta)$ of Definition~\ref{def:prelim-R} directly from the daily fixings and uses the SARB period averages only as a check on day-count and business-day handling.

\datanote{The proxy history before November 2022 is what the MPG fallback methodology used from 4~January 2016 \cite{sarb2025fallback} and what the Derivatives Workstream used from the ``beginning of 2016'' \cite{sarb2025ois}. Whether the full proxy series from January 2016 is exportable from the SARB selector, or only the observation-period series from November 2022, was not verified on 9~September 2026: one commercial commentary states that part of the five-year window ``relies on proxy history that is not publicly available''. The reproducibility protocol must record the earliest date the selector returns. If proxy fixings before November 2022 are needed and are not exportable, request them from \texttt{sarb-benchmarks@resbank.co.za}; failing that, the thesis uses the 2016--2022 proxy only through the SARB synthetic curve of Section~\ref{sec:appC-synthetic}, which embeds it.}

\subsection{SARB synthetic historical ZARONIA OIS curve}\label{sec:appC-synthetic}

\emph{Free.} The SARB Market Practitioners Group's Derivatives Workstream published \emph{Historical estimation of the ZARONIA OIS curve} on the SARB site on 9~October 2025 (document dated 6~October 2025) \cite{sarb2025ois}, with five Excel binary workbooks (\texttt{.xlsb}): \texttt{Benchmark\_Rates}; \texttt{Forecast\_Curve\_Expiry\_Dates} and \texttt{Forecast\_Curve\_\allowbreak Discount\_Factors}; \texttt{Discount\_Curve\_Payment\_Dates} and \texttt{Discount\_Curve\_\allowbreak Discount\_Factors}. Landing page and paper:
\begin{quote}\footnotesize\raggedright\url{https://www.resbank.co.za/en/home/publications/publication-detail-pages/Financial-Markets/Committees/MPG/MPG-Related-pages/2025/historical-estimation-of-the-zaronia-ois-curve}
\end{quote}
Coverage and construction, as stated in the paper: the input is the JSE's daily bootstrapped nominal swap zero curve (deposits, FRAs and swaps on 3-month JIBAR), available from 3~January 2005 to 31~December 2024 with four missing dates (24~Dec~2007, 2~Jan~2009, 24~Nov~2011, 21~May~2013). Daily 3-month JIBAR forward rates are read off that curve and shifted by a tenor-dependent spread equal to the trailing median of the spot 3-month JIBAR minus ZARONIA spread over a window matching the forward's tenor; ZARONIA is the SARB series from 2016 and the SAFEX overnight rate from 2000 to 2015; the spread history therefore runs from 2000, the curves from 2005. The shifted forwards are compounded into discount factors and par rates are produced for spot-starting single-period OIS from 1 to 12 months in monthly steps and spot-starting multi-period OIS from 15 months to 30 years in 3-month steps. The Workstream describes the dataset as ``a convenient, but optional, risk management resource'' built for VaR and expected-shortfall scenarios.

Two consequences for Chapter~\ref{ch7-zar-coda}. First, the synthetic curve is a deterministic function of the JIBAR curve and a median spread; its JIBAR--ZARONIA basis has, by construction, no daily volatility beyond that of the rolling median, so it must not be used to estimate $\eta$, $\rho_{BF}$ or $\rho_{B\alpha}$ of Chapter~\ref{ch5-identifiability}. Second, it is adequate, and is the only public source, for the pre-2025 discount factors $P(t,T+\Delta)$ and forwards $F_t$ that the converted-book P\&L of Section~\ref{sec:appC-pnl} needs before a live ZARONIA OIS market existed.

\subsection{JIBAR fixings, 1M/3M/6M/12M}\label{sec:appC-jibar}

\emph{Free.} SARB is the JIBAR administrator; its JIBAR page exports the fixing history as CSV, XML or PDF through the same selector mechanism as ZARONIA:
\begin{quote}\footnotesize\raggedright\url{https://www.resbank.co.za/en/home/what-we-do/statistics/johannesburg-interbank-average-rate}
\end{quote}
The JSE client portal keeps the longer archive under \emph{Downloadable Files~$\to$ Safex~$\to$ mtmdata}: a current \texttt{jbarRates.xls}, one folder per year from 2010 to 2026, and a ``Jibar Archive'' folder:
\begin{quote}\footnotesize\raggedright\url{https://clientportal.jse.co.za/downloadable-files?RequestNode=%2Fsafex%2Fmtmdata}
\end{quote}
JIBAR is published in 1-, 3-, 6- and 12-month tenors; a 9-month tenor also has a fixed fallback spread (Section~\ref{sec:appC-cas}). JIBAR ceases after its final publication on 31~December 2026 \cite{isda2025}, so the fixing series the thesis uses is closed at that date and the JSE archive is the source of record for the full 2000--2026 history that the five-year CAS window and the Workstream's spread history draw on.

\subsection{ZARONIA OIS and JIBAR swap curves}\label{sec:appC-curves}

\emph{Free to end-2024; terminal from 2025.} For dates up to 31~December 2024 the JIBAR swap curve is the JSE nominal swap zero curve (daily, in the same \texttt{mtmdata} tree as the JIBAR files) and the ZARONIA OIS curve is the Workstream's synthetic curve of Section~\ref{sec:appC-synthetic}. From January 2025 the live ZARONIA OIS market (cleared at LCH since September 2024, listed on Bloomberg SEF from 13~March 2025) is quoted on the terminals, and the thesis needs end-of-day par OIS rates at the Workstream's pillars (1M--12M monthly, 15M--30Y quarterly) together with the JIBAR swap curve until JIBAR pricing screens close. The two curves give $F_t$ and $L_t$ for every accrual window and the discount factors for every payment date.

\datanote{Bloomberg curve identifiers and LSEG RICs for the ZARONIA OIS and JIBAR swap curves were not found in any public document on 9~September 2026 and are to be confirmed at the terminal before download; the BISL fallback tickers in Table~\ref{tab:appC-cas} are the only ZAR identifiers verified from a primary source. The JSE nominal swap zero curve continues after 2024 and is free; whether the JSE will publish a ZARONIA-based zero curve, and from what date, was not verified. A commercial provider (BlueGamma) advertises a downloadable ZARONIA forward curve rebuilt from live quotes; it is not a primary source and the thesis does not rely on it.}

\subsection{JIBAR cap/floor and swaption volatility surfaces}\label{sec:appC-vols}

\emph{Sponsor-only.} No public or terminal source of ZAR cap, floor or swaption volatilities exists that the thesis can cite: the JIBAR cap market is quoted by dealers, not on a screen, and no evidence of quoted ZARONIA cap, floor or swaption screens was found as of 9~September 2026 (this is stated as absence of evidence, not proof of absence, and is re-checked before submission). The Market Practitioners Group's conventions paper for ZARONIA non-linear derivatives \cite{sarb2024conv} says these products ``will in future be quoted on screens''.

What a sponsoring dealer would provide, under a data agreement that restricts publication to aggregates:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
\item End-of-day JIBAR cap/floor implied volatilities, normal (basis-point) quotation, for each business day from a start date agreed with the sponsor to 31~December 2026: one CSV per date with columns \emph{expiry}, \emph{strike} (absolute rate or offset from the at-the-money forward), \emph{normal volatility}, \emph{quote type} (mid, or bid and ask), \emph{source} (desk mark or consensus).
\item The same for ZARONIA caps and floors from the first date the desk marks them.
\item A swaption cube (expiry $\times$ tail $\times$ strike offset) in the same layout, JIBAR-referenced until cessation and ZARONIA-referenced thereafter.
\item The desk's own strike and expiry grids and interpolation rule, so that the thesis reproduces the desk's surface rather than resampling it.
\end{enumerate}
The thesis publishes no raw quote. Chapter~\ref{ch7-zar-coda} reports (i) fitted SABR triples $(\alpha_0,\rho,\nu)$ per expiry, (ii) at-the-money volatility and skew differences between the JIBAR and ZARONIA surfaces in basis points, and (iii) monthly averages of these, none of which recovers a dealer's quote. The agreement should permit exactly these three aggregates and should be signed by month~12 of the programme (the ground-truth timetable places the sponsor decision at months 10--12).

\subsection{Converted-book hedging P\&L from public fixings}\label{sec:appC-pnl}

\emph{Free.} The score $S$ of Chapter~3 is the hedging error of a fixed policy $\pi$ over a block of rebalancing periods. For the South African coda the policy is the market convention, fixed before cessation: a JIBAR caplet with strike $K$ becomes a compounded caplet with strike $K'=K-s$, and its volatility is the pre-cessation JIBAR volatility rescaled through the clock of Assumption~\ref{ass:prelim-linear}. Everything needed to compute the realised P\&L of a delta-hedged position under that policy is public:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
\item the realised compounded rate $R(T,T+\Delta)$ from the daily ZARONIA fixings (Section~\ref{sec:appC-zaronia});
\item the JIBAR fixing $J_T$ for windows that fixed before cessation (Section~\ref{sec:appC-jibar});
\item the constant $s=0.1619\%$ (Section~\ref{sec:appC-cas});
\item the forwards $F_t$, $L_t$ and discount factors $P(t,T+\Delta)$ from the curves of Section~\ref{sec:appC-curves} at each monthly rebalancing date;
\item one volatility level per expiry, frozen at the last date a JIBAR surface is available.
\end{enumerate}
Item~5 is the only input that is not free. Without a sponsor the frozen level is replaced by the realised volatility of the compounded forward over the calibration window, and the policy is then a historical-volatility policy rather than the market-convention policy. The coverage certificate of Chapter~3 (ground-truth Theorem~6) is indifferent to which policy is used, since it requires only that $\pi$ be fixed on $\Dfit$; the finance interpretation of the reserve decomposition in Chapter~\ref{ch7-zar-coda} is what changes. The rebalancing period is one month, the block length $b$ is chosen by the rule of Chapter~\ref{ch4-sharpness-blocking}, and the record runs from the first monthly date after the LCH discounting switch of 11~April 2026 to the last date before submission, giving at most about twenty-four monthly scores by month~30 as the ground truth anticipates.

\section{Code architecture}
\subsection{The \texttt{rates} package}\label{subsec:appC-rates}

The multi-curve bootstrap and the time-changed SABR caplet pricer of Chapter~\ref{ch:prelim} are implemented as a small Python package, \texttt{thesis/code/rates/}, with a test file \texttt{thesis/code/tests/} \texttt{test\_rates.py}. The package depends on \texttt{numpy} only; the normal distribution function is taken from the standard library (\texttt{math.erf}), so no \texttt{scipy} installation is needed. The code is a reference implementation of the formulas of Chapter~\ref{ch:prelim}, not a production library: it has three modules and no calibration routine. Calibration to USD and ZAR quotes is the subject of the code described in the sections that follow, which is not yet written.

\paragraph{\texttt{curve.py}: discount curve and forward compounded rate.}
\texttt{bootstrap\_ois} returns discount factors $P(0,t_i)$ at increasing pillar maturities $t_1<\dots<t_n$ from the par rates $R_i$ of overnight index swaps maturing at $t_i$. The floating leg of an OIS is worth $1-P(0,t_i)$ at inception; the fixed leg is taken to pay $R_i(t_j-t_{j-1})$ at every pillar $t_j\le t_i$, so that the bootstrap is the closed recursion
\[
P(0,t_i)=\frac{1-R_i\sum_{j<i}(t_j-t_{j-1})P(0,t_j)}{1+R_i\,(t_i-t_{i-1})},
\]
with no root finding. The convention that fixed coupons fall on the pillar grid is exact when the pillars beyond one year are annual, which is the case for the curves used in Chapter~\ref{ch:usd}; a general coupon schedule would replace the annuity sum and nothing else. \texttt{discount} interpolates $\log P(0,t)$ linearly in $t$ between pillars, that is, with a constant continuously compounded forward rate on each interval, with $P(0,0)=1$ at the short end and a flat forward beyond the last pillar. \texttt{forward\_compounded} returns $F_0=(P(0,T)/P(0,T+\Delta)-1)/\Delta$, the identity $1+\Delta F_0=P(0,T)/P(0,T+\Delta)$ of Section~\ref{sec:prelim-compounded} under the continuous approximation.

\paragraph{\texttt{clock.py}: decay profile and clock.}
\texttt{g\_linear} is the profile of Assumption~\ref{ass:prelim-linear}, $g(t)=1$ for $t\le T$ and $(T+\Delta-t)/\Delta$ on $(T,T+\Delta]$. \texttt{tau} computes the clock $\tau(t)=\int_0^tg(u)^2\,du$ by composite Simpson quadrature on $[T,t]$, adding $T$ for the part where $g=1$. The value $\tau(T+\Delta)=T+\Delta/3$ is deliberately not hard-coded: it is a consequence of the linear-decay assumption, and the test verifies it numerically to $10^{-12}$ (Simpson's rule is exact for the quadratic $g^2$, so the agreement is to rounding). Any other profile can be passed as \texttt{g}; the pricer then uses its clock, and the factor $\Delta/3$ is replaced by whatever $c_g$ the profile implies, as in Example~\ref{ex:prelim-cg}.

\paragraph{\texttt{sabr.py}: Hagan volatility, Bachelier price, two caplet variants.}
\texttt{hagan\_normal\_vol} is formula \eqref{eq:prelim-hagan-N}, reducing to \eqref{eq:prelim-hagan-N0} at $\beta=0$; at $\zeta=0$ it uses $\zeta/x(\zeta)=1$. \texttt{bachelier\_call} is the undiscounted Bachelier call of Definition~\ref{def:prelim-implied}. \texttt{caplet\_time\_changed} is Lemma~\ref{lem:prelim-tc} in code: the Hagan volatility at the clock $\tau(T+\Delta)$, the Bachelier price at that clock, times $P(0,T+\Delta)$, per unit accrual. Both the compounded and the JIBAR caplet are priced under $\Q^{T+\Delta}$, and the code contains no convexity adjustment. \texttt{caplet\_nondecaying\_volvol} is the variant of Proposition~\ref{prop:prelim-novol} and is labelled in the source as an approximation, because no closed form exists for time-dependent vol-of-vol. Its two accountings are the two of the gap box after Proposition~\ref{prop:prelim-nueff}: \texttt{accounting="total"} uses the ground-truth identification $\nu_{\mathrm{eff}}^2=\nu^2(T+\Delta)/\tau^*$ with $\rho$ unchanged; \texttt{accounting="hlw"} uses the effective parameters \eqref{eq:prelim-eff} computed by \texttt{hlw\_effective}. The latter evaluates the two integrals of \eqref{eq:prelim-eff} in calendar time through the substitution $du=g^2\,dt$, under which $\tilde\nu(u)^2(\tau^*-u)^2\,du$ becomes $\nu^2(\tau^*-\tau(t))^2\,dt$ and $\tilde\nu(u)(\tau^*-u)\,du$ becomes $\nu\,g(t)(\tau^*-\tau(t))\,dt$; the $w^{-2/3}$ singularity of \eqref{eq:prelim-nutilde-linear} disappears and Simpson quadrature suffices.

\paragraph{Tests and what they reproduce.}
The test file runs under \texttt{pytest} or as a plain script. It checks: that the bootstrap returns $P(0,n)=(1+r)^{-n}$ on a flat annual curve and reprices its inputs to $10^{-14}$; that log-linear interpolation keeps a flat curve flat between and beyond the pillars; that $\tau(T+\Delta)=T+\Delta/3$ to $10^{-12}$ for two $(T,\Delta)$ pairs and that $\tau(t)=T+\tfrac\Delta3(1-g(t)^3)$ inside the window; that the at-the-money Bachelier price equals $\sigma\sqrt t/\sqrt{2\pi}$ to $10^{-16}$ and the deep-in and deep-out-of-the-money limits are the intrinsic value and zero; and that the worked example of Section~\ref{subsec:prelim-worked} is reproduced. With $T=1$, $\Delta=0.25$, $F_0=K=7\%$, $\alpha_0=0.006$, $\beta=0$, $\nu=0.4$, $\rho=-0.2$ the code returns $\tau^*=1.083333$, an at-the-money normal volatility of $60.815$ basis points at the clock and $56.615$ quoted at $T+\Delta$, wings of $63.79$ and $59.90$ basis points at $\mp50$ basis points, a caplet price per unit accrual and notional of $0.0025252$ against $0.0027181$ for constant-parameter SABR run to $T+\Delta$ without decay, a reduction of $7.1\%$. On the two vol-of-vol accountings the volatility differences from Lemma~\ref{lem:prelim-tc} at $[-50,\,0,\,+50]$ basis points are $[+0.418,\,+0.125,\,+0.147]$ basis points for the ground-truth accounting ($\nu_{\mathrm{eff}}=0.42967$), a curvature effect, wing lift net of the at-the-money lift, of $0.157$ basis points; and $[+0.0023,\,-0.0000,\,-0.0023]$ basis points for the effective-parameter accounting ($\nu_{\mathrm{eff}}^2=1.00013\,\nu^2$, $\rho_{\mathrm{eff}}=-0.20022$), a curvature effect below $10^{-5}$ basis points. The test asserts the closed forms $\nu_{\mathrm{eff}}^2/\nu^2=1+\tfrac{2}{567}\Delta^3/(\tau^{*3}/3)$ and $\rho_{\mathrm{eff}}\nu_{\mathrm{eff}}/(\rho\nu)=(T\tau^*-T^2/2+\Delta^2/15)/(\tau^{*2}/2)$ to $10^{-9}$, which fixes the quadrature as well as the formulas.

\datanote{The last figures refine one sentence of the gap box after Proposition~\ref{prop:prelim-nueff}: on the effective-parameter accounting the wing differences are about $0.002$ basis points, not below $0.001$, because $\rho_{\mathrm{eff}}$ moves by $0.11\%$ and tilts the skew; the curvature difference, which is the quantity the proposition is about, is below $10^{-5}$ basis points. The conclusion of the gap box is unchanged: the variant choice is immaterial for pricing at this order, the decay is not. Run: \texttt{cd thesis/code \&\& python3 tests/test\_rates.py} (or \texttt{python3 -m pytest tests/}); six tests, all passing on 9 September 2026 with Python 3.14 and numpy 2.4 and with Python 3.12 under pytest.}
