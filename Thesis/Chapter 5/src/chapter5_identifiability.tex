\chapter{Identifiability of the Successor-Rate Smile}\label{ch5-identifiability}\label{ch:identifiability}

\begin{quote}\itshape
This chapter asks what the volatility smile of a JIBAR caplet can and cannot reveal about the volatility smile of the compounded-ZARONIA caplet that replaces it at cessation. Both instruments pay at the end of the accrual window and are therefore priced under one measure, so no convexity adjustment separates them; what separates them is the tenor basis. Writing the JIBAR forward as the compounded forward plus the basis plus the fixed spread, the JIBAR caplet is expanded in the dimensionless basis scale. The observed JIBAR smile is a contaminated view of the successor smile: its level is shifted through the basis--rate correlation, its skew is tilted through the basis--volatility correlation, and its curvature is moved through both. The contamination lies in a two-dimensional family, so the strike shift by the spread plus the forward basis, together with the time change to the clock $T+\Delta/3$, is identified, while two correlation products are not. The consequence is proved sharply: the diffusion coefficient of the successor rate at the clock is identified within an interval of half-width exactly equal to the basis volatility, as an identity in the basis scale, and both endpoints are attained by explicit models; the corresponding half-width of the quoted volatility differs from that by a computed factor of order the squared vol-of-vol. A parameter count gives a deficit of three, or two at first order in the basis scale. A worked example at illustrative South African parameters shows the interval to be a third of the at-the-money level and wide enough to contain a successor smile with no skew at all; the one published estimate of the basis dynamics does not pin the basis volatility to within an order of magnitude, so that fraction is itself bracketed rather than known. What remains open is a uniform remainder bound in the basis scale, whose numerical value is not small in this market, and the case of a basis with a term structure of volatility.
\end{quote}

\section{The question, and why it is identifiability and not calibration}\label{sec:ident-question}

\subsection{The problem}

At the close of business on 31 December 2026 the JIBAR fixing is published for the last time: publication of all remaining tenors ceases immediately after their final publication on that date \citep[p.~1]{isda2025}. Every JIBAR-linked cap, floor and swaption that incorporates the fallback provisions then falls back to compounded ZARONIA plus a fixed credit adjustment spread $s$, the successor being the all-in Fallback Rate (ZARONIA) with a spread fixed as the five-year historical median of the difference between JIBAR and compounded ZARONIA \citep[Sects.~2 and~4]{isda2025}; the transition record, including which legacy contracts convert mechanically and which do not, is Chapter~\ref{ch:intro}. The day before, the bank marks that book against a JIBAR volatility surface that has been quoted for thirty years. The day after, it must mark the same book against a compounded-ZARONIA volatility surface that has never been quoted at all. No screen for ZARONIA caps, floors or swaptions was found as of the date of the survey recorded in Chapter~\ref{ch:intro}; the market conventions paper anticipates such quotes rather than reporting them \citep[\S8, p.~26]{sarb2024conv}.

The bank's question is therefore not how to fit a model to successor-rate option prices. There are none. The question is whether the successor-rate smile is \emph{determined} by what the bank can see: the legacy JIBAR smile, the two curves that give today's forward basis, and the published spread. If it is determined, the transition is a change of variables and the bank's marks are a computation. If it is not determined, the bank is choosing among observationally equivalent models, and it should know the size of the set it is choosing from before it chooses.

\subsection{Identifiability rather than calibration}

Calibration and identifiability are different questions, and conflating them is the standard error in transition work.

Calibration is a numerical problem. Given a parametric family, an observed set of prices generated by some member of the family, and a loss function, calibration finds the parameter that minimises the loss. It presupposes that the parameter is determined by the prices; its difficulties are conditioning, local minima and stability under re-marking. A calibration is judged by the fit residual.

Identifiability is a structural property of the map from parameters to observables, prior to any data and to any numerical method. A functional of the parameter is identified when it takes the same value at every parameter consistent with the observables. When it is not identified, no amount of data of the observed kind, no regularisation and no improvement in the optimiser will recover it: the deficiency is in the experiment, not in the estimator. A non-identified functional can still be reported, but only as an interval, and the interval must be derived, not fitted.

\begin{definition}[Identified functional and identified set]\label{def:ident-identified}
Let $\Theta$ be a parameter set and let $\Lambda:\Theta\to\mathcal O$ be the map sending a parameter to the collection of observables it generates. For $\vartheta\in\Theta$ the \emph{identified set at $\vartheta$} is
\[
\Theta(\vartheta):=\{\vartheta'\in\Theta:\Lambda(\vartheta')=\Lambda(\vartheta)\}.
\]
A functional $h:\Theta\to\R$ is \emph{identified at $\vartheta$} if $h$ is constant on $\Theta(\vartheta)$, and \emph{identified within an interval of half-width $w$} if
$\tfrac12\big(\sup_{\Theta(\vartheta)}h-\inf_{\Theta(\vartheta)}h\big)\le w$, with equality when the bound is attained. The model is identified if $\Lambda$ is injective.
\end{definition}

Definition~\ref{def:ident-identified} is the standard set-identification formulation and is stated in full here because the chapter needs the interval-valued version of it.

The distinction matters here for a practical reason. A desk that treats the transition as a calibration will fit a SABR triple to the JIBAR smile, shift the strike by the spread, apply the market's volatility-decay convention \citep[\S8.3.2, eqs~(15)--(16), p.~29]{sarb2024conv}, and report the result as the successor smile with a fit residual of a fraction of a basis point. The residual is genuine and the procedure is defensible. What the residual does not measure is the distance between the reported smile and the true one, because the fitted triple is not the true triple: it is the true triple contaminated by the basis, and the contamination is invisible in the fit. Theorem~\ref{thm:ident} makes the contamination explicit and Theorem~\ref{thm:ident-two} shows that it cannot be removed by any use of the observables.

\subsection{Why the basis and not the convexity}

Two mechanisms could in principle separate the JIBAR caplet from the compounded caplet. The first is a difference of payment date. A rate fixed in advance and paid in advance must be re-expressed under the payment-date measure, which introduces a convexity term. That mechanism is absent here: the JIBAR caplet fixes at $T$ and pays at $T+\Delta$, and the compounded caplet also pays at $T+\Delta$, so both are expectations under $\Q^{T+\Delta}$ of functions of $\Q^{T+\Delta}$-martingales (Remark~\ref{rem:prelim-no-convexity} of Chapter~\ref{ch:prelim}).

One qualification is needed and is quantified rather than passed over. Under the market convention the successor caplet settles not at the end of the accrual window but two business days later, at $T(i)=T_i+2$ business days, the lag being recommended to resolve the calculation lag rather than to adjust the fixing \citep[\S6.1, p.~8 and \S7.6, p.~24]{sarb2024conv}. Its price is therefore a $\Q^{T+\Delta+\delta_p}$-expectation with $\delta_p$ the lag, and $F$ acquires a drift under that measure equal to minus its covariation with the logarithm of the num\'eraire ratio $P(\cdot,T+\Delta)/P(\cdot,T+\Delta+\delta_p)$. That log-ratio is, to first order, $\delta_p$ times the forward rate over the stub $[T+\Delta,T+\Delta+\delta_p]$, so the induced shift in $\E F_{T+\Delta}$ is bounded in modulus by $\alpha_0\,\sigma_{\mathrm{stub}}\,\delta_p\,(T+\Delta)$. Taking $\sigma_{\mathrm{stub}}$ of the same order as $\alpha_0=60$ basis points and $\delta_p\le4$ calendar days, the bound is $0.005$ basis points, against a quote precision of $0.01$ basis points. What this chapter therefore asserts is that no convexity term of the in-advance/in-arrears kind appears anywhere in it, and that the payment-lag term is below quote precision at the order worked to; it is not carried in any formula below.
\datanote{The two-business-day payment lag is the recommendation of \citet[\S6.1, p.~8 and \S7.6, p.~24]{sarb2024conv} for ZARONIA-based non-linear derivatives. The bound $0.005$ basis points uses $\alpha_0=60$ basis points for both the rate volatility and the volatility of the two-day stub forward, $\delta_p=4/365$ years and $T+\Delta=1.25$ years, and takes the correlation at its extreme value one; the volatility of a two-day stub forward is not separately estimated anywhere in this thesis.}

The second mechanism is the tenor basis $B_t=L_t-F_t-s$ of Definition~\ref{def:prelim-fallback}. It is a genuine second stochastic object: the JIBAR forward is not a ratio of traded discount bonds and cannot be replicated from them. The basis is a $\Q^{T+\Delta}$-martingale (Proposition~\ref{prop:prelim-B-mart}), so its pricing-measure mean at expiry, $B_0=L_0-F_0-s$, is read off the two curves with no model. Its volatility and its correlations with the drivers of the compounded rate are not.

The direction of the contamination is the point of the chapter and is easily got backwards. The compounded caplet is written on $F$ alone, so its price depends on the SABR triple $(\alpha_0,\rho,\nu)$ and on nothing else. The JIBAR caplet is written on $L=F+B+s$, so its price depends on the triple \emph{and} on the law of the basis. The unobservable nuisance therefore sits in the instrument we can see, not in the instrument we want. The target smile is clean; the observed smile is dirty. Identification runs from a contaminated observation to a clean target, which is why it fails in a specific, computable, two-dimensional way rather than failing altogether.

\section{Model, observables and admissible parameters}\label{sec:ident-model}

\subsection{Standing assumptions}

Throughout the chapter we work under the $(T+\Delta)$-forward measure $\Q^{T+\Delta}$ of Definition~\ref{def:prelim-forward-measure} on a single accrual window $[T,T+\Delta]$, with the discount curve built from ZARONIA overnight index swaps as in Section~\ref{subsec:prelim-multicurve}. We take $\beta=0$ throughout, so every smile is a normal SABR smile in the sense of Definition~\ref{def:prelim-sabr}, and normal (Bachelier) implied volatility is the quoting convention, as the market conventions paper mandates \citep[\S8, p.~26]{sarb2024conv}. Assumption~\ref{ass:prelim-linear}, linear decay of the compounded-rate volatility inside the accrual window, is in force, so the clock of Definition~\ref{def:prelim-decay} ends at $\tau^*:=\tau(T+\Delta)=T+\Delta/3$ by Proposition~\ref{prop:prelim-clock-linear}. All three are assumptions and every result below inherits them; Remark~\ref{rem:prelim-linear-status} of Chapter~\ref{ch:prelim} records their status and Appendix~\ref{app:tenor} records what they cost across tenors.

The dynamics are those of \eqref{eq:prelim-sabr-decay} and Definition~\ref{def:prelim-basis-dyn}. On $[0,T+\Delta]$, under $\Q^{T+\Delta}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ident-dyn}
dF_t=g(t)\,\alpha_t\,dW_t,\qquad d\alpha_t=g(t)\,\nu\,\alpha_t\,dZ_t,\qquad dB_t=\eta\,dW^B_t\ \ (t\le T),
\end{equation}
with $d\langle W,Z\rangle_t=\rho\,dt$, $d\langle W^B,W\rangle_t=\rho_{BF}\,dt$, $d\langle W^B,Z\rangle_t=\rho_{B\alpha}\,dt$, and with $g\equiv1$ on $[0,T]$. The basis lives only on $[0,T]$, because $L$ is fixed at $T$. Two consequences are used constantly and are worth stating once. On $[0,T]$ the decay profile is identically one, so the pair $(F,\alpha)$ follows \emph{constant-parameter} SABR there and the JIBAR caplet, which expires at $T$, sees no time change at all. On $[0,T+\Delta]$ the compounded caplet sees the full clock and, by Lemma~\ref{lem:prelim-tc}, is priced as a constant-parameter SABR caplet at expiry $\tau^*$, quoted against calendar time $T+\Delta$ by Corollary~\ref{cor:prelim-quoted}.

The dimensionless basis scale of Definition~\ref{def:prelim-basis-dyn} specialises at $\beta=0$ to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ident-eps}
\varepsilon:=\frac{\eta}{\alpha_0},
\end{equation}
the basis volatility measured in units of the rate volatility. It is the parameter in which everything below is expanded. It is dimensionless because both $\eta$ and $\alpha_0$ are normal volatilities; the quantity $\eta\sqrt T$ is a rate, not a small parameter.

\subsection{The observable set}

\begin{definition}[Observable set]\label{def:ident-observable}
Fix a strike grid $\mathcal K\subset\R$. The \emph{observable set} for the window $[T,T+\Delta]$ is
\[
\Sset:=\big\{\sigma^N_L(K,T):K\in\mathcal K\big\}\ \cup\ \{B_0\}\ \cup\ \{s\},
\]
where $\sigma^N_L(K,T)$ is the normal implied volatility, quoted against the expiry $T$, of the JIBAR caplet with strike $K$ priced by \eqref{eq:prelim-jibar-caplet}; $B_0=L_0-F_0-s$ is today's forward basis, read off the JIBAR and ZARONIA curves by \eqref{eq:prelim-EB}; and $s$ is the fixed credit adjustment spread. The \emph{target} is the successor smile
\[
\sigma^N_F(K',T+\Delta),\qquad K'=K-s,
\]
the normal implied volatility of the compounded caplet \eqref{eq:prelim-compounded-caplet}, quoted against calendar time $T+\Delta$ as in \eqref{eq:prelim-quoted}.
\end{definition}

Three remarks on the definition. First, the fallback strike is $K'=K-s$ because $(R+s-K)^+=(R-K')^+$ identically (Definition~\ref{def:prelim-fallback}); the strike shift is exact, not an approximation, and it is exact for any $\beta$, though only for $\beta=0$ does it act as a pure translation of the smile. Second, the recommended grid is thirteen strikes per expiry, $\{K\pm2\%,\pm1.5\%,\pm1\%,\pm0.75\%,\pm0.5\%,\pm0.25\%,K\}$ \citep[\S7.6, p.~25]{sarb2024conv}; the count in Section~\ref{sec:ident-count} uses this. Third, the set contains no successor-rate option price; that is the whole premise.

\subsection{Admissible parameters}

The parameter is
\[
\vartheta=(\alpha_0,\rho,\nu,\eta,\rho_{BF},\rho_{B\alpha}),\qquad \alpha_0>0,\ \nu>0,\ \rho\in(-1,1),\ \eta\ge0.
\]
Not every correlation triple is admissible: three Brownian motions with pairwise correlations $(\rho,\rho_{BF},\rho_{B\alpha})$ exist only if the correlation matrix is positive semi-definite. The constraint binds throughout and is proved once.

\begin{lemma}[Admissible correlation triples]\label{lem:ident-psd}
Let $|\rho|<1$ and set
\[
C(\rho,\rho_{BF},\rho_{B\alpha}):=\begin{pmatrix}1&\rho&\rho_{BF}\\ \rho&1&\rho_{B\alpha}\\ \rho_{BF}&\rho_{B\alpha}&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
\begin{enumerate}[nosep,label=(\roman*)]
\item $C$ is positive semi-definite if and only if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ident-admiss}
\rho_{BF}^2-2\rho\,\rho_{BF}\rho_{B\alpha}+\rho_{B\alpha}^2\ \le\ 1-\rho^2 .
\end{equation}
\item Whenever \eqref{eq:ident-admiss} holds, a Brownian triple $(W,Z,W^B)$ with those correlations exists on a suitable space; and \eqref{eq:ident-admiss} is invariant under $(\rho_{BF},\rho_{B\alpha})\mapsto-(\rho_{BF},\rho_{B\alpha})$.
\item Writing $v:=(v_1,v_2):=\eta(\rho_{BF},\rho_{B\alpha})$, condition \eqref{eq:ident-admiss} is $v_1^2-2\rho v_1v_2+v_2^2\le\eta^2(1-\rho^2)$, and in the coordinates $x:=v_1$, $y:=v_2-2\rho v_1$ it reads
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ident-ellipse}
x^2+2\rho xy+y^2\ \le\ \eta^2(1-\rho^2).
\end{equation}
The set $E_\eta$ of admissible $v$ is a closed ellipse, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ident-extremes}
\max_{v\in E_\eta}|v_1|=\max_{v\in E_\eta}|v_2|=\max_{v\in E_\eta}|v_2-2\rho v_1|=\eta,
\end{equation}
each maximum being attained.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(i) Write $C$ in block form with leading block $M=\begin{psmallmatrix}1&\rho\\ \rho&1\end{psmallmatrix}$ and off-diagonal column $c=(\rho_{BF},\rho_{B\alpha})^{\!\top}$. Since $|\rho|<1$, $M$ is positive definite with $M^{-1}=(1-\rho^2)^{-1}\begin{psmallmatrix}1&-\rho\\ -\rho&1\end{psmallmatrix}$. We claim $C\succeq0$ if and only if the Schur complement $1-c^{\!\top}M^{-1}c\ge0$. Indeed, with $S:=\begin{psmallmatrix}I_2&M^{-1}c\\ 0&1\end{psmallmatrix}$, which is unit upper triangular and hence invertible, a direct multiplication gives the congruence
\[
C=S^{\!\top}\begin{pmatrix}M&0\\ 0&1-c^{\!\top}M^{-1}c\end{pmatrix}S ,
\]
and congruence by an invertible matrix preserves the signs of the eigenvalues, since $x^{\!\top}Cx=(Sx)^{\!\top}D(Sx)$ with $D$ the block-diagonal matrix and $x\mapsto Sx$ a bijection of $\R^3$. As $M\succ0$, the block-diagonal matrix is positive semi-definite exactly when its last entry is non-negative, which is the claim. Expanding, $c^{\!\top}M^{-1}c=(\rho_{BF}^2-2\rho\rho_{BF}\rho_{B\alpha}+\rho_{B\alpha}^2)/(1-\rho^2)$, which gives \eqref{eq:ident-admiss}.

(ii) If $C\succeq0$ take its Cholesky-type factor $C=LL^{\!\top}$ and set $(W,Z,W^B)^{\!\top}=L\,(\tilde W_1,\tilde W_2,\tilde W_3)^{\!\top}$ for independent Brownian motions $\tilde W_i$; the quadratic covariations are then the entries of $C$. Explicitly one may take $W=\tilde W_1$, $Z=\rho\tilde W_1+\sqrt{1-\rho^2}\,\tilde W_2$ and $W^B=c_1\tilde W_1+c_2\tilde W_2+c_3\tilde W_3$ with $c_1=\rho_{BF}$, $c_2=(\rho_{B\alpha}-\rho\rho_{BF})/\sqrt{1-\rho^2}$ and $c_3=\sqrt{1-c_1^2-c_2^2}$, the last being real precisely by \eqref{eq:ident-admiss}. Invariance under a joint sign flip is immediate because every term in \eqref{eq:ident-admiss} is of even total degree in $(\rho_{BF},\rho_{B\alpha})$.

(iii) The first display is \eqref{eq:ident-admiss} multiplied by $\eta^2$. Substituting $v_2=y+2\rho x$,
\[
v_1^2-2\rho v_1v_2+v_2^2=x^2-2\rho x(y+2\rho x)+(y+2\rho x)^2=x^2+2\rho xy+y^2,
\]
the terms $-4\rho^2x^2$ and $+4\rho^2x^2$ cancelling, which is \eqref{eq:ident-ellipse}. For the extremes, let $A:=\begin{psmallmatrix}1&-\rho\\ -\rho&1\end{psmallmatrix}$, so that the constraint is $v^{\!\top}Av\le\kappa$ with $\kappa=\eta^2(1-\rho^2)$, and let $c\in\R^2$. Substituting $v=A^{-1/2}w$ and applying the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality,
\[
\max_{v^{\!\top}Av\le\kappa}c^{\!\top}v=\max_{\|w\|^2\le\kappa}(A^{-1/2}c)^{\!\top}w=\sqrt{\kappa\,c^{\!\top}A^{-1}c},
\]
attained at $v=\sqrt{\kappa/(c^{\!\top}A^{-1}c)}\,A^{-1}c$. With $A^{-1}=(1-\rho^2)^{-1}\begin{psmallmatrix}1&\rho\\ \rho&1\end{psmallmatrix}$ one computes $c^{\!\top}A^{-1}c=(c_1^2+2\rho c_1c_2+c_2^2)/(1-\rho^2)$, which equals $1/(1-\rho^2)$ for each of $c=(1,0)$, $c=(0,1)$ and $c=(-2\rho,1)$: in the last case $4\rho^2-4\rho^2+1=1$. Hence each maximum is $\sqrt{\eta^2(1-\rho^2)/(1-\rho^2)}=\eta$.
\end{proof}

The three coincident extremes in \eqref{eq:ident-extremes} are not a coincidence; they are the reason the interval half-widths of Theorem~\ref{thm:ident-two} come out as clean multiples of $\eta$. The maximiser of $|v_1|$ is $v=\pm\eta(1,\rho)$, that is $\rho_{BF}=\pm1$ with $\rho_{B\alpha}=\pm\rho$: the basis is then driven by the rate's own Brownian motion, and its correlation with the volatility is forced to be that of the rate.

\section{The expansion in the basis scale}\label{sec:ident-expansion}

The observed JIBAR caplet is an option on $L_T=F_T+B_T+s$. Since $L$ is a $\Q^{T+\Delta}$-martingale on $[0,T]$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:prelim-L-mart}) and $g\equiv1$ there, the strategy is: compute the first three cumulants of $L_T$, identify the normal SABR triple at expiry $T$ with the same three cumulants, and read the smile off that triple by Proposition~\ref{prop:prelim-lsc}. The device of matching cumulants and quoting the constant-parameter formula at the matched values is the one used for time-dependent SABR coefficients in Proposition~\ref{prop:prelim-nueff}, following \citep[App.~B]{hagan2002}; it is used here for a different perturbation, the additive basis, with the same justification and the same order of accuracy.

\subsection{Three cumulants of the JIBAR forward}

\begin{lemma}[Cumulants of the JIBAR forward at expiry]\label{lem:ident-cumulants}
Under \eqref{eq:ident-dyn} write $X:=L_T-L_0=(F_T-F_0)+(B_T-B_0)$ and set
\[
\theta:=\alpha_0+\rho_{BF}\,\eta .
\]
Then $\E^{\Q^{T+\Delta}}[X]=0$ and:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep,label=(\roman*)]
\item (Second cumulant, exact.)
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ident-kappa2}
\kappa_2(X)=\Var(X)=\alpha_0^2\,\frac{e^{\nu^2T}-1}{\nu^2}+2\rho_{BF}\,\eta\,\alpha_0T+\eta^2T
=\big(\alpha_0^2+2\rho_{BF}\alpha_0\eta+\eta^2\big)T+O(\nu^2T^2).
\end{equation}
\item (Third cumulant, leading order in $\nu$.)
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ident-kappa3}
\kappa_3(X)=3\,\alpha_0\,\nu\,\theta\,\big(\alpha_0\rho+\eta\rho_{B\alpha}\big)\,T^2+O(\nu^2).
\end{equation}
\item (Variance of the realised variance, leading order in $\nu$.) With $\langle L\rangle_T=\int_0^T(\alpha_u^2+2\rho_{BF}\eta\alpha_u+\eta^2)\,du$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ident-varV}
\Var\big(\langle L\rangle_T\big)=\tfrac43\,\alpha_0^2\,\theta^2\,\nu^2\,T^3+O(\nu^3).
\end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
All three reduce to the constant-parameter normal SABR values $\alpha_0^2T$, $3\alpha_0^3\rho\nu T^2$ and $\tfrac43\alpha_0^4\nu^2T^3$ when $\eta=0$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
On $[0,T]$ we have $g\equiv1$, so $X=\int_0^T\alpha_u\,dW_u+\eta W^B_T$, a sum of two stochastic integrals with square-integrable integrands; both have zero mean, so $\E[X]=0$ and the central moments are the cumulants up to third order.

(i) By the It\^o isometry and the covariance formula for stochastic integrals against correlated Brownian motions \citep[Ch.~3, pp.~128--238]{karatzas1991},
\[
\Var(X)=\E\int_0^T\alpha_u^2\,du+2\rho_{BF}\eta\,\E\int_0^T\alpha_u\,du+\eta^2T .
\]
The volatility is the geometric Brownian motion $\alpha_u=\alpha_0\exp(\nu Z_u-\tfrac12\nu^2u)$ of Definition~\ref{def:prelim-sabr}, so $\E[\alpha_u]=\alpha_0$ and $\E[\alpha_u^2]=\alpha_0^2e^{\nu^2u}$; integrating gives $\alpha_0^2(e^{\nu^2T}-1)/\nu^2$ and $\alpha_0T$ respectively, which is the first equality in \eqref{eq:ident-kappa2}. Expanding $(e^{\nu^2T}-1)/\nu^2=T+\tfrac12\nu^2T^2+O(\nu^4T^3)$ gives the second.

(ii) Expand the stochastic exponential to first order: $\alpha_u=\alpha_0(1+\nu Z_u)+O(\nu^2)$ in $L^2$, uniformly on $[0,T]$. Hence
\[
X=Y+V+O(\nu^2),\qquad Y:=\alpha_0W_T+\eta W^B_T,\qquad V:=\alpha_0\nu\int_0^TZ_u\,dW_u .
\]
$Y$ is centred Gaussian, so $\E[Y^3]=0$; $\E[YV^2]$ and $\E[V^3]$ are $O(\nu^2)$ and $O(\nu^3)$. Therefore $\kappa_3(X)=\E[X^3]=3\,\E[Y^2V]+O(\nu^2)$.

Put $Y_t:=\alpha_0W_t+\eta W^B_t$, a Brownian motion with variance rate $\sigma_Y^2:=\alpha_0^2+2\rho_{BF}\alpha_0\eta+\eta^2$ and with $d\langle Y,W\rangle_t=(\alpha_0+\eta\rho_{BF})\,dt=\theta\,dt$. By It\^o's formula \citep[Thm.~3.3.6]{karatzas1991}, $Y_T^2=2\int_0^TY_u\,dY_u+\sigma_Y^2T$, and the deterministic part contributes nothing to $\E[Y_T^2V]$ because $\E[V]=0$. The covariance formula for two stochastic integrals then gives
\[
\E[Y_T^2V]=2\alpha_0\nu\,\E\Big[\int_0^TY_u\,dY_u\int_0^TZ_u\,dW_u\Big]=2\alpha_0\nu\int_0^T\E[Y_uZ_u]\,\theta\,du .
\]
Now $\E[Y_uZ_u]=\alpha_0\E[W_uZ_u]+\eta\E[W^B_uZ_u]=(\alpha_0\rho+\eta\rho_{B\alpha})u$, so
\[
\E[Y_T^2V]=2\alpha_0\nu\theta(\alpha_0\rho+\eta\rho_{B\alpha})\int_0^Tu\,du=\alpha_0\nu\theta(\alpha_0\rho+\eta\rho_{B\alpha})T^2,
\]
and multiplying by $3$ gives \eqref{eq:ident-kappa3}.

(iii) The quadratic variation of $L=F+B+s$ on $[0,T]$ is $\langle L\rangle_T=\int_0^T(\alpha_u^2+2\rho_{BF}\eta\alpha_u+\eta^2)du$, the middle term coming from $d\langle F,B\rangle_u=\rho_{BF}\alpha_u\eta\,du$. Substituting $\alpha_u=\alpha_0(1+\nu Z_u)+O(\nu^2)$ gives $\alpha_u^2=\alpha_0^2(1+2\nu Z_u)+O(\nu^2)$, so the fluctuating part is
\[
\langle L\rangle_T-\E\langle L\rangle_T=\big(2\alpha_0^2+2\rho_{BF}\eta\alpha_0\big)\nu\int_0^TZ_u\,du+O(\nu^2)=2\alpha_0\theta\nu\int_0^TZ_u\,du+O(\nu^2).
\]
Since $\int_0^TZ_u\,du=\int_0^T(T-u)\,dZ_u$ by the product rule (no covariation term, $u$ being deterministic), its variance is $\int_0^T(T-u)^2du=T^3/3$, and \eqref{eq:ident-varV} follows.
\end{proof}

Each of the three cumulants carries the basis differently, and the pattern is the content of the chapter. The second cumulant sees the basis through the variance of a sum: the cross term $2\rho_{BF}\eta\alpha_0T$ is first order in $\eta$ and the own term $\eta^2T$ is second order. The third cumulant sees it twice, once through $\theta=\alpha_0+\rho_{BF}\eta$, which is the instantaneous covariation of $L$ with $W$, and once through $\alpha_0\rho+\eta\rho_{B\alpha}$, which is the covariation of $L$ with the volatility driver: the second of these is the $\Cov(B,\alpha)$ channel. The variance of the realised variance sees only $\theta$, because the basis has no volatility of its own volatility.

\subsection{The contamination map}

\begin{proposition}[Contamination map]\label{prop:ident-map}
Let $(a,r,n)$ be the normal SABR triple at expiry $T$, forward $L_0$, whose second and third cumulants and whose variance of realised variance agree at leading order in $\nu$ with those of $L_T$ in Lemma~\ref{lem:ident-cumulants}. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ident-map}
a=\sqrt{\alpha_0^2+2\rho_{BF}\alpha_0\eta+\eta^2},\qquad
r=\frac{\alpha_0\rho+\eta\rho_{B\alpha}}{a},\qquad
n=\frac{\alpha_0\,\theta\,\nu}{a^2},\qquad \theta=\alpha_0+\rho_{BF}\eta,
\end{equation}
and consequently $a^2n=\alpha_0\theta\nu$ and $rn=\alpha_0\theta\nu(\alpha_0\rho+\eta\rho_{B\alpha})/a^3$. To first order in $\varepsilon=\eta/\alpha_0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ident-map1}
a=\alpha_0\big(1+\rho_{BF}\varepsilon\big)+O(\varepsilon^2\alpha_0),\quad
r=\rho+\varepsilon\big(\rho_{B\alpha}-\rho\rho_{BF}\big)+O(\varepsilon^2),\quad
n=\nu\big(1-\rho_{BF}\varepsilon\big)+O(\varepsilon^2\nu),
\end{equation}
and hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ident-rn1}
rn=\nu\Big[\rho+\varepsilon\big(\rho_{B\alpha}-2\rho\rho_{BF}\big)\Big]+O(\varepsilon^2\nu).
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For a constant-parameter normal SABR model with triple $(a,r,n)$ at expiry $T$, Lemma~\ref{lem:ident-cumulants} at $\eta=0$ gives $\kappa_2=a^2T$, $\kappa_3=3a^3rnT^2$ and $\Var\langle\cdot\rangle_T=\tfrac43a^4n^2T^3$. Matching each to the corresponding expression in \eqref{eq:ident-kappa2}--\eqref{eq:ident-varV} at leading order in $\nu$ gives
\[
a^2=\alpha_0^2+2\rho_{BF}\alpha_0\eta+\eta^2,\qquad
a^3rn=\alpha_0\nu\theta(\alpha_0\rho+\eta\rho_{B\alpha}),\qquad
a^4n^2=\alpha_0^2\theta^2\nu^2 .
\]
The first is the first formula of \eqref{eq:ident-map}. The third gives $a^2n=\alpha_0\theta\nu$ on taking positive roots, since $a,n,\alpha_0,\nu>0$ and $\theta>0$ whenever $\varepsilon<1$; this is the third formula. Dividing the second by $n=\alpha_0\theta\nu/a^2$ gives
\[
r=\frac{\alpha_0\nu\theta(\alpha_0\rho+\eta\rho_{B\alpha})}{a^3}\cdot\frac{a^2}{\alpha_0\theta\nu}=\frac{\alpha_0\rho+\eta\rho_{B\alpha}}{a},
\]
the second formula. For \eqref{eq:ident-map1}, note $a^2=\theta^2+\eta^2(1-\rho_{BF}^2)$, so $a=\theta+O(\varepsilon^2\alpha_0)=\alpha_0(1+\rho_{BF}\varepsilon)+O(\varepsilon^2\alpha_0)$. Then
\[
r=\frac{\alpha_0(\rho+\rho_{B\alpha}\varepsilon)}{\alpha_0(1+\rho_{BF}\varepsilon)}+O(\varepsilon^2)=(\rho+\rho_{B\alpha}\varepsilon)(1-\rho_{BF}\varepsilon)+O(\varepsilon^2)=\rho+\varepsilon(\rho_{B\alpha}-\rho\rho_{BF})+O(\varepsilon^2),
\]
and $n=\alpha_0\theta\nu/a^2=\nu\alpha_0/\theta+O(\varepsilon^2\nu)=\nu(1+\rho_{BF}\varepsilon)^{-1}+O(\varepsilon^2\nu)=\nu(1-\rho_{BF}\varepsilon)+O(\varepsilon^2\nu)$. Multiplying the last two expansions gives \eqref{eq:ident-rn1}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Two exact checks]\label{rem:ident-checks}
Formula \eqref{eq:ident-map} can be verified without any expansion in two cases.

Take $\rho_{BF}=1$ and $\rho_{B\alpha}=\rho$, which is admissible by \eqref{eq:ident-admiss} with equality. Then $W^B=W$ and the JIBAR forward satisfies $dL_t=(\alpha_t+\eta)\,dW_t$ exactly. Its initial volatility is $\alpha_0+\eta$, which is \eqref{eq:ident-map} because $a^2=(\alpha_0+\eta)^2$; its correlation with the volatility driver is that of $W$ with $Z$, namely $\rho$, which is \eqref{eq:ident-map} because $(\alpha_0\rho+\eta\rho)/(\alpha_0+\eta)=\rho$; and its relative volatility of volatility is, from $d(\alpha_t+\eta)=\nu\alpha_t\,dZ_t$, equal to $\nu\alpha_0/(\alpha_0+\eta)$ at $t=0$, which is \eqref{eq:ident-map} because $\alpha_0\theta\nu/a^2=\alpha_0(\alpha_0+\eta)\nu/(\alpha_0+\eta)^2$. Every entry of the map is reproduced.

Take $\rho_{BF}=\rho_{B\alpha}=0$, a basis independent of both drivers. Then $a=\sqrt{\alpha_0^2+\eta^2}$, $r=\rho\alpha_0/a$ and $n=\nu\alpha_0^2/a^2$: all three are perturbed at $O(\varepsilon^2)$ and none at $O(\varepsilon)$. An independent basis is invisible to first order, which is why $\eta$ alone is identified only at second order.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[What the map says]\label{rem:ident-reading}
Read \eqref{eq:ident-map1} together with Proposition~\ref{prop:prelim-lsc}, which says that the level, skew and curvature of a normal SABR smile are $\alpha_0c_t$, $\rho\nu c_t/2$ and $(2-3\rho^2)\nu^2c_t/(6\alpha_0)$.

The \emph{level} of the observed JIBAR smile exceeds the true $\alpha_0$ by $\rho_{BF}\eta$ at first order. The mechanism is the variance of a sum: the cross term $2\rho_{BF}\eta\alpha_0T$ in \eqref{eq:ident-kappa2} is first order in the basis volatility, and the square root converts it into an additive shift of the volatility. A basis positively correlated with the rate makes the JIBAR smile look more volatile than the compounded rate is.

The \emph{skew} of the observed smile is tilted, at first order, by $\tfrac12\nu\varepsilon(\rho_{B\alpha}-2\rho\rho_{BF})$ per unit of strike. The $\rho_{B\alpha}$ part is the covariance channel: a basis correlated with the volatility driver imports a rate--volatility correlation that the compounded rate does not have. The $\rho_{BF}$ part is the reflex of the level shift, since the skew is a ratio of the third cumulant to the cube of the level.

The \emph{curvature} is moved as well, through $n=\nu(1-\rho_{BF}\varepsilon)$ and through $r$. Nothing new enters: both movements are proportional to the same two products $\rho_{BF}\eta$ and $\rho_{B\alpha}\eta$ that move the level and the skew. This is the structural fact behind the theorem. The contamination is not three-dimensional but two-dimensional, and it acts on the smile through exactly two functions of strike.
\end{remark}

\datanote{The closed forms \eqref{eq:ident-map} were checked against a Monte Carlo simulation of \eqref{eq:ident-dyn} on $[0,T]$ with $T=1$, $\alpha_0=0.006$, $\nu=0.4$, $\rho=-0.2$, $\eta=0.002$, $\rho_{BF}=0.3$, $\rho_{B\alpha}=-0.2$, that is at $\varepsilon=1/3$. Euler discretisation with $4000$ steps, $2\times10^6$ paths, common random numbers between the run with $\eta=0$ and the run with $\eta=0.002$, and the three matched quantities extracted from the simulated sample. The $a$ entry needs no simulation at all and is better checked in closed form: retaining $(e^{\nu^2T}-1)/\nu^2$ instead of $T$ in \eqref{eq:ident-kappa2} gives the exact ratio
\[
\Big[\frac{\alpha_0^2(e^{\nu^2T}-1)/\nu^2+2\rho_{BF}\eta\alpha_0T+\eta^2T}{\alpha_0^2(e^{\nu^2T}-1)/\nu^2}\Big]^{1/2}=1.13441 ,

\section{The decomposition theorem}\label{sec:ident-thm3}

\begin{theorem}[Identified and unidentified components of the smile map]\label{thm:ident}
Let \eqref{eq:ident-dyn} hold with Assumption~\ref{ass:prelim-linear} and $\beta=0$, let $\nu\neq0$, and let $(\rho_{BF},\rho_{B\alpha})$ satisfy \eqref{eq:ident-admiss}. Let $\sigma^N_L(\cdot,T)$ and $\sigma^N_F(\cdot,T+\Delta)$ be as in Definition~\ref{def:ident-observable}, and write $q:=\sqrt{\tau^*/(T+\Delta)}$ and $c^*:=1+\tfrac1{24}(2-3\rho^2)\nu^2\tau^*$. Then, with $u:=K'-F_0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ident-decomp}
\sigma^N_F(K',T+\Delta)\;=\;\Phi_0\big(K';\sigma^N_L(\cdot,T),s,B_0,\Delta\big)\;-\;\rho_{BF}\,\eta\,\Phi_1(K')\;-\;\rho_{B\alpha}\,\eta\,\nu\,\Phi_2(K')\;+\;O(\varepsilon^2),
\end{equation}
where
\begin{enumerate}[nosep,label=(\roman*)]
\item $\Phi_0$ is the normal SABR smile at clock $\tau^*=T+\Delta/3$, quoted against $T+\Delta$ by \eqref{eq:prelim-quoted}, whose triple $(a,r,n)$ is fitted to $\sigma^N_L(\,\cdot+s+B_0,\,T)$ at clock $T$: a strike shift by $s+B_0$ followed by the time change of Lemma~\ref{lem:prelim-tc};
\item $\Phi_1$ is a parallel level shift,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ident-phi1}
\Phi_1(K')=q\,c^*\Big[1-\frac{\rho\nu}{\alpha_0}\,u-\frac{(2-5\rho^2)\nu^2}{4\alpha_0^2}\,u^2+O(u^3)\Big]=1+O(u);
\end{equation}
\item $\Phi_2$ is a skew tilt,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ident-phi2}
\Phi_2(K')=q\,c^*\,\frac{u}{2\alpha_0}\Big[1-\frac{\rho\nu}{\alpha_0}\,u+O(u^2)\Big]\ \propto\ (K'-F_0).
\end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
The functions $\Phi_1$ and $\Phi_2$ are the $\varepsilon$-derivatives of the smile; at $\varepsilon=1/3$ the $O(\varepsilon^2)$ term in the skew coefficient exceeds the $O(\varepsilon)$ term, so the first-order part of \eqref{eq:ident-decomp} is a guide to magnitude and not to sign at that value, as Remark~\ref{rem:ident-epsilon} shows numerically; the at-the-money conclusion below is unaffected because it is exact in $\varepsilon$.

Consequently the strike shift, the time change and the map $\Phi_0$ are identified from the observable set $\Sset$ of Definition~\ref{def:ident-observable}; the pair $(\eta\rho_{BF},\eta\rho_{B\alpha})$ is not identified at $O(\varepsilon)$; and the triple $(\eta,\rho_{BF},\rho_{B\alpha})$ is not identified at $O(\varepsilon^2)$. In particular the diffusion coefficient of the successor rate at the clock $\tau^*$ is identified only within an interval of half-width $\eta$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{route}
Three steps.

\emph{Step 1: the observed smile is a SABR smile with a contaminated triple.} On $[0,T]$ the decay profile is identically one, so the JIBAR caplet is an option at expiry $T$ on the martingale $L_T=F_T+B_T+s$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:ident-cumulants} and Proposition~\ref{prop:ident-map}, the second and third cumulants of $L_T$ and the variance of its realised variance coincide, at leading order in $\nu$, with those of a normal SABR model with the triple $(a,r,n)$ of \eqref{eq:ident-map}. Its smile is therefore \eqref{eq:prelim-hagan-N0}, that is the Hagan normal-volatility formula \citep[eq.~(A.67a) with $\beta=0$]{hagan2002}, evaluated at $(a,r,n)$ and expiry $T$; this is the object that a desk fits to $\sigma^N_L(\cdot,T)$, and the fit returns $(a,r,n)$ and not $(\alpha_0,\rho,\nu)$. The strike shift is exact: $\sigma^N_L(K,T)$ as a function of $K$ becomes a function of $K'=K-s$ centred at $L_0-s=F_0+B_0$, so shifting the argument by $s+B_0$ recentres the smile on $F_0$, and $B_0$ is observable by \eqref{eq:prelim-EB}.

\emph{Step 2: push the triple through the time change.} By Lemma~\ref{lem:prelim-tc} the compounded caplet is a constant-parameter SABR caplet at clock $\tau^*$ with the \emph{true} triple $(\alpha_0,\rho,\nu)$, and Corollary~\ref{cor:prelim-quoted} converts it to a volatility quoted against $T+\Delta$ by the factor $q$. Applying the same two operations to the fitted triple defines $\Phi_0$. Thus $\Phi_0$ and $\sigma^N_F$ are the same function of strike evaluated at two different triples, and their difference is the difference of triples propagated through the smile.

\emph{Step 3: propagate the difference of triples.} Expanding \eqref{eq:prelim-hagan-N0} by Lemma~\ref{lem:prelim-zx-series} at clock $\tau^*$ and quoting by $q$,
\[
\sigma(K';\alpha_0,\rho,\nu)=q\Big[\alpha_0c^*+\frac{c^*\rho\nu}{2}u+\frac{c^*(2-3\rho^2)\nu^2}{12\alpha_0}u^2+O(u^3)\Big].
\]
Substituting the first-order map \eqref{eq:ident-map1} and \eqref{eq:ident-rn1} and subtracting, the coefficient of $u^0$ is $\alpha_0-a=-\rho_{BF}\eta$; the coefficient of $u^1$ is $\tfrac12(\rho\nu-rn)=-\tfrac{\eta\nu}{2\alpha_0}(\rho_{B\alpha}-2\rho\rho_{BF})$; and the coefficient of $u^2$ is
\[
\frac{1}{12\alpha_0}\Big[(2-3\rho^2)\nu^2-\frac{\alpha_0}{a}(2-3r^2)n^2\Big]
=\frac{\varepsilon\nu^2}{12\alpha_0}\Big[3\rho_{BF}(2-5\rho^2)+6\rho\rho_{B\alpha}\Big]+O(\varepsilon^2),
\]
using $(2-3r^2)=(2-3\rho^2)-6\rho\varepsilon(\rho_{B\alpha}-\rho\rho_{BF})$, $n^2=\nu^2(1-2\rho_{BF}\varepsilon)$ and $\alpha_0/a=1-\rho_{BF}\varepsilon$. Collecting the terms proportional to $\rho_{BF}\eta$ into $-\rho_{BF}\eta\Phi_1$ and those proportional to $\rho_{B\alpha}\eta$ into $-\rho_{B\alpha}\eta\nu\Phi_2$ produces \eqref{eq:ident-phi1} and \eqref{eq:ident-phi2}; every remaining term is a product of two factors of $\varepsilon$ and is absorbed into $O(\varepsilon^2)$.

The identification statements follow. $\Phi_0$ is a function of the observables alone, hence identified. The two products $(\eta\rho_{BF},\eta\rho_{B\alpha})$ enter $\Sset$ at $O(\varepsilon)$ only through the same two combinations that they contribute to \eqref{eq:ident-decomp}, so moving them along the ellipse $E_\eta$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:ident-psd} changes the successor smile while leaving the observed smile unchanged to $O(\varepsilon^2)$; this is proved in Theorem~\ref{thm:ident-two}. The volatility $\eta$ enters $\Sset$ on its own only through the term $\eta^2T$ of \eqref{eq:ident-kappa2} and the $O(\varepsilon^2)$ perturbations of Remark~\ref{rem:ident-checks}, so $(\eta,\rho_{BF},\rho_{B\alpha})$ separately is not identified at that order either. The at-the-money statement is $\Phi_1(F_0)=qc^*$ together with $\max_{E_\eta}|v_1|=\eta$ from \eqref{eq:ident-extremes}, and is sharpened to an exact statement in Theorem~\ref{thm:ident-two}.
\end{route}

\gap{Three items remain to be established for Theorem~\ref{thm:ident}.
\textbf{(a) Remainder bounds.} The symbol $O(\varepsilon^2)$ in \eqref{eq:ident-decomp} is a formal order statement obtained by truncating two expansions: the expansion of the cumulants in $\nu$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:ident-cumulants}(ii)--(iii), and the expansion of the smile in $u=K'-F_0$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:prelim-zx-series}. What is not established is a bound of the form $|{\cdot}|\le C(\nu,T,|u|)\,\varepsilon^2$ with an explicit constant, uniform over the strike grid and over the admissible ellipse, nor the joint treatment of that remainder with the $O(\alpha^2\tau)$ error already carried by the Hagan approximation (Theorem~\ref{thm:prelim-hagan}). Section~\ref{sec:ident-example} shows that at $\varepsilon=1/3$ the neglected terms change the first-order skew tilt by more than its own size, so the bound is not a formality. A third uniformity is required and is the hard one: $\varepsilon=\eta/\alpha_0$ is a ratio at time zero, but $\eta$ is constant while $\alpha_t$ is a lognormal martingale, so the instantaneous ratio $\eta/\alpha_t$ is random. At the worked parameters $\nu=0.4$ and $T=1$ its median is $0.361$ and its interquartile range is $[0.276,0.473]$ around a time-zero value of $1/3$. A uniform remainder bound must therefore hold in expectation over the law of $\alpha$, or on an event whose probability is stated with the complement bounded; and the natural small parameter may be $\eta/\alpha_t$ rather than $\eta/\alpha_0$.
\textbf{(b) A basis with a term structure of volatility.} Proposition~\ref{prop:ident-termstructure} gives the first-order level shift for a deterministic profile $\eta(\cdot)$, but the corresponding first-order skew and curvature coefficients, the analogue of the ellipse \eqref{eq:ident-ellipse}, and the exact interval of Theorem~\ref{thm:ident-two} in that case are not established. Nor is the change of measure that would take a basis mean-reverting under the physical measure, as estimated by \citet[Sec.~5.1, Table~4]{alfeus2026}, to a specific $\eta(\cdot)$ under $\Q^{T+\Delta}$; the basis must be a $\Q^{T+\Delta}$-martingale by Proposition~\ref{prop:prelim-B-mart}, so physical mean reversion can appear only as a volatility profile, and which profile is not derived here.
\textbf{(c) Information in the far strikes.} The three matched cumulants make the law of $L_T$ agree with a SABR law to the order at which the smile is read. The law of $L_T$ is not exactly a SABR law, and the departure is in principle informative about $(\rho_{BF},\rho_{B\alpha})$ through the fourth and higher cumulants. The order in $\varepsilon$ and in $\nu$ at which that departure appears, and hence whether the thirteen-strike grid of \citep[\S7.6, p.~25]{sarb2024conv} narrows the identified set at all, is not established.
\textbf{(d) A basis with jumps.} The basis is continuous throughout, and the only published dynamic study of the South African spread models it with jumps on monetary-policy dates \citep[Abstract]{alfeus2026}. A jump component breaks Lemma~\ref{lem:ident-cumulants} at third order: $\kappa_3(X)$ acquires a term in the third moment of the jump measure which is proportional to neither $\rho_{BF}\eta$ nor $\rho_{B\alpha}\eta$. The two-dimensionality of the contamination, which is the structural fact the whole chapter rests on, is therefore not established for a jumping basis, and this is the one open item that could change the \emph{dimension} of the deficit rather than its size.
\textbf{(e) A lognormal or shifted-lognormal quote.} The exponent $\beta=0$ is fixed in the standing assumptions and defended by the normal quoting convention, but Black volatility is one of the three mandated quote formats and the historical South African one \citep[\S8, p.~26]{sarb2024conv}. The strike shift $(R+s-K)^+=(R-K')^+$ is exact for every $\beta$, but only at $\beta=0$ does it translate the smile. For $\beta>0$ the basis is still additive in the rate while the diffusion coefficient $\alpha_tF_t^\beta$ is not, so the cumulant computation of Lemma~\ref{lem:ident-cumulants} goes through with $F$-dependent coefficients while the map from cumulants to the Hagan triple no longer decouples; whether the contamination is still two-dimensional in that case is not established.}

\begin{proposition}[Basis with a deterministic volatility profile: the level shift]\label{prop:ident-termstructure}
Replace the third equation of \eqref{eq:ident-dyn} by $dB_t=\eta(t)\,dW^B_t$ with $\eta(\cdot)$ deterministic, bounded and non-negative on $[0,T]$, and set
\[
\bar\eta:=\frac1T\int_0^T\eta(t)\,dt .
\]
Then \eqref{eq:ident-kappa2} becomes $\kappa_2(X)=\alpha_0^2(e^{\nu^2T}-1)/\nu^2+2\rho_{BF}\alpha_0\bar\eta T+\int_0^T\eta(t)^2dt$, so the level of the observed smile is $a=\alpha_0+\rho_{BF}\bar\eta+O(\bar\eta^2/\alpha_0)$ and the successor at-the-money volatility is identified within an interval of half-width $\bar\eta$ at first order. In particular, for the exponential profile $\eta(t)=\eta\,e^{-\kappa(T-t)}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ident-h}
\bar\eta=\eta\,h(\kappa T),\qquad h(x):=\frac{1-e^{-x}}{x},\qquad h(0)=1,\ h\ \text{decreasing}.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:ident-cumulants}(i) is unchanged except that $\eta$ is inside the integrals: $\Var(B_T)=\int_0^T\eta(t)^2dt$ by the It\^o isometry, and $\Cov(F_T,B_T)=\rho_{BF}\E\int_0^T\alpha_t\eta(t)\,dt=\rho_{BF}\alpha_0\int_0^T\eta(t)\,dt$ because $\E[\alpha_t]=\alpha_0$ and $\eta$ is deterministic. Dividing by $T$ and taking the square root as in Proposition~\ref{prop:ident-map} gives $a=\alpha_0+\rho_{BF}\bar\eta+O(\bar\eta^2/\alpha_0)$. The half-width statement is $\max|\rho_{BF}|=1$, admissible by Lemma~\ref{lem:ident-psd}. For \eqref{eq:ident-h}, $\int_0^Te^{-\kappa(T-t)}dt=(1-e^{-\kappa T})/\kappa$, and dividing by $T$ gives $h(\kappa T)$; $h$ is decreasing on $(0,\infty)$ because $x\mapsto1-e^{-x}$ is concave with value $0$ at $0$.
\end{proof}

Proposition~\ref{prop:ident-termstructure} is the quantitative form of the failure mode. A basis whose volatility runs off before expiry contributes less to the observed level, so the identified interval is narrower by the factor $h(\kappa T)$; the statement survives with a modified constant, and it never widens. What the proposition does not do is give the corresponding skew and curvature coefficients, which is item (b) of the gap box.

\section{Two observationally equivalent models}\label{sec:ident-thm4}

Theorem~\ref{thm:ident} says that two combinations are missing. This section shows that they are genuinely missing, by exhibiting pairs of admissible models that agree on everything observable and disagree on the target. For the diffusion coefficient at the clock the result is exact in $\varepsilon$, not merely first order, and both endpoints of the identified interval are attained by models one can write down in a line.

\subsection{The inverse map}

\begin{proposition}[Inverse of the contamination map]\label{prop:ident-inverse}
Fix an observed triple $(a,r,n)$ with $a>0$, $|r|<1$, $n>0$, and a basis volatility $\eta$ with $a>\eta\sqrt2$. For each $p:=\rho_{BF}\in[-1,1]$ and $\rho_{B\alpha}\in\R$ define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ident-inverse}
\theta(p):=\sqrt{a^2-\eta^2(1-p^2)},\qquad
\alpha_0(p):=\theta(p)-p\eta,\qquad
\nu:=\frac{n\,a^2}{\alpha_0(p)\,\theta(p)},\qquad
\rho:=\frac{ra-\eta\rho_{B\alpha}}{\alpha_0(p)} .
\end{equation}
Then $(\alpha_0,\rho,\nu,\eta,p,\rho_{B\alpha})$ solves \eqref{eq:ident-map} with the given $(a,r,n)$, and every solution with $\alpha_0>0$ arises this way. The map $p\mapsto\alpha_0(p)$ is strictly decreasing on $[-1,1]$ with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ident-alpharange}
\alpha_0(1)=a-\eta,\qquad \alpha_0(-1)=a+\eta,\qquad \alpha_0\big([-1,1]\big)=[a-\eta,\,a+\eta].
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Solve the first equation of \eqref{eq:ident-map} for $\alpha_0$ at fixed $(a,\eta,p)$. It is the quadratic $\alpha_0^2+2p\eta\alpha_0+(\eta^2-a^2)=0$ with roots $-p\eta\pm\sqrt{p^2\eta^2-\eta^2+a^2}$; only the root with the $+$ sign is positive when $a>\eta$, and it is $\alpha_0(p)$ as displayed, whence $\theta=\alpha_0+p\eta=\theta(p)$. Substituting into the third and second equations of \eqref{eq:ident-map} and solving for $\nu$ and $\rho$ gives the last two formulas of \eqref{eq:ident-inverse}. Conversely any solution has $\theta=\alpha_0+p\eta$ and $a^2=\theta^2+\eta^2(1-p^2)$, so $\theta=\theta(p)$ and $\alpha_0=\alpha_0(p)$.

For monotonicity, differentiate: $\alpha_0'(p)=\eta^2p/\theta(p)-\eta=\eta\,(\eta p/\theta(p)-1)$. Since $\theta(p)\ge\sqrt{a^2-\eta^2}$ and $a>\eta\sqrt2$ gives $\sqrt{a^2-\eta^2}>\eta$, we have $|\eta p/\theta(p)|\le\eta/\sqrt{a^2-\eta^2}<1$, so $\alpha_0'(p)<0$ on $[-1,1]$. The endpoint values are $\theta(\pm1)=a$ and $\alpha_0(\pm1)=a\mp\eta$, and $\alpha_0$ is continuous, so its range is exactly $[a-\eta,a+\eta]$.
\end{proof}

The proposition also records the constraints that a candidate must satisfy to be a model at all: $\alpha_0(p)>0$, $|\rho|<1$, and the admissibility condition \eqref{eq:ident-admiss} evaluated at the derived $\rho$. The next theorem checks all three at the endpoints.

\subsection{The theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Two models]\label{thm:ident-two}
Let $(a,r,n)$ be the triple fitted to an observed JIBAR smile at expiry $T$, let $\eta>0$ satisfy $a>\eta\sqrt2$ and $|r|<\sqrt{1-\eta^2/a^2}$, and let $B_0$ and $s$ be given. Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item (Exact statement for the diffusion coefficient at the clock.) For every $w\in[-\eta,\eta]$ there is an admissible model with basis volatility $\eta$ that reproduces the fitted triple $(a,r,n)$ exactly, matches $B_0$ exactly, and whose diffusion coefficient at the clock $\tau^*$ is $\alpha_0=a-w$; its successor at-the-money volatility at the clock is then $(a-w)\,c^*(\rho,\nu)$ with the Hagan bracket of Theorem~\ref{thm:ident} evaluated at that model's own $(\rho,\nu)$. The extremes are attained by
\[
\mathcal M_+:\ dL_t=(\alpha_t+\eta)\,dW_t\quad(\rho_{BF}=1,\ \rho_{B\alpha}=\rho),\qquad
\mathcal M_-:\ dL_t=(\alpha_t-\eta)\,dW_t\quad(\rho_{BF}=-1,\ \rho_{B\alpha}=-\rho),
\]
with $\alpha_0^{\pm}=a\mp\eta$, $\rho^{\pm}=r$ and $\nu^{\pm}=na/(a\mp\eta)$. Hence $\alpha_0$ is identified within an interval of half-width exactly $\eta$, the endpoints being attained. In the quoted convention the two endpoint models carry different vol-of-vol and therefore different Hagan brackets, and the quoted half-width is not $\eta qc^*$ but
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ident-quotedhw}
\tfrac12\big(\Sigma^--\Sigma^+\big)
=\eta\,q\Big[1-\frac{(2-3r^2)n^2\tau^*}{24}\cdot\frac{a^2}{a^2-\eta^2}\Big]
=\eta\,q\,c^*\Big[1-\frac{(2-3r^2)n^2\tau^*}{12}+O\big(\nu^2\tau^*(\varepsilon^2+\nu^2\tau^*)\big)\Big],
\end{equation}
where $\Sigma^{\pm}:=\alpha_0^{\pm}c^{*\pm}q$ with $c^{*\pm}:=1+\tfrac1{24}(2-3r^2)(\nu^{\pm})^2\tau^*$. The relative correction is $O(\nu^2\tau^*)$ and does not vanish with $\eta$: the second term of $\alpha_0^{\pm}c^{*\pm}=\alpha_0^{\pm}+(2-3r^2)n^2a^2\tau^*/(24\alpha_0^{\pm})$ is decreasing in $\alpha_0^{\pm}$ and so offsets part of the spread of the first. At the parameters of Section~\ref{sec:ident-example} the exact quoted half-width is $18.4290$ basis points against $\eta qc^*=18.7929$, a relative difference of $-1.94\%$.
\item (First-order statement at every strike.) For every $v=(v_1,v_2)$ in the ellipse $E_\eta$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:ident-psd} there are admissible models $\mathcal M_{\pm v}$ with $(\eta\rho_{BF},\eta\rho_{B\alpha})=\pm(v_1,v_2)$ that match every JIBAR caplet to $O(\varepsilon^2)$ and match $B_0$ exactly, and whose successor smiles satisfy, at every strike,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ident-twodiff}
\sigma^{N,-v}_F(K',T+\Delta)-\sigma^{N,+v}_F(K',T+\Delta)=2v_1\Phi_1(K')+2v_2\,\nu\,\Phi_2(K')+O(\varepsilon^2).
\end{equation}
Their at-the-money volatilities differ by $2v_1\,q\,c^*$ and their skews by $q\,c^*\,(\nu/\alpha_0)(v_2-2\rho v_1)$; over $E_\eta$ the first ranges over $[-2\eta qc^*,\,2\eta qc^*]$ and the second over $[-\eta qc^*\nu/\alpha_0,\,\eta qc^*\nu/\alpha_0]$, so each successor quantity is identified within an interval of half-width $\eta\,qc^*$ and $\eta\,qc^*\nu/(2\alpha_0)$ respectively.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

Three senses of the word \emph{exact} occur in this chapter and are separated once here. Exactness in $\varepsilon$ means that a formula is an identity in the basis scale with no truncation: \eqref{eq:ident-map}, \eqref{eq:ident-inverse}, \eqref{eq:ident-alpharange} and \eqref{eq:ident-homotopy} are exact in this sense. Exactness as a model identity means that two expressions are equal pathwise, as in the strike shift $(R+s-K)^+=(R-K')^+$. Exactness of a match means the following, and it is the sense used in part (i):
\begin{quote}
\emph{Throughout, ``the fitted triple is reproduced exactly'' means as an identity in $\varepsilon$ at the leading order in $\nu$ at which the triple $(a,r,n)$ is defined by Proposition~\ref{prop:ident-map}. The three matched quantities are the cumulant functionals $\kappa_2(L_T)$, $\kappa_3(L_T)$ and $\Var\langle L\rangle_T$, not observables: the observables are the caplet prices. Two models that agree on $(a,r,n)$ therefore agree on the JIBAR caplet prices only up to the $O(\nu^2)$ cumulant error of Lemma~\ref{lem:ident-cumulants}(ii)--(iii) and the error of the Hagan approximation (Theorem~\ref{thm:prelim-hagan}).}
\end{quote}
For the two extreme models of part (i) the first of those two errors is smaller than the general bound. Because $\nu^{\pm}=na/\alpha_0^{\pm}$, the product $\alpha_0^{\pm}\nu^{\pm}=na$ is the \emph{same} for $\mathcal M_+$ and $\mathcal M_-$, so the leading neglected term of the exact second cumulant \eqref{eq:ident-kappa2}, namely $\tfrac12\alpha_0^2\nu^2T^2=\tfrac12(na)^2T^2$, is identical for the two models and cancels in their difference. Their second cumulants therefore agree to $O(\nu^4)$ and not merely to $O(\nu^2)$.

\begin{proof}
\emph{(i).} Take $\mathcal M_+$ first. Set $\rho_{BF}=1$ and $\rho_{B\alpha}=r$. Condition \eqref{eq:ident-admiss} then reads $1-2r\cdot1\cdot r+r^2\le1-r^2$, that is $1-r^2\le1-r^2$: it holds with equality, so the triple is admissible and, by Lemma~\ref{lem:ident-psd}(ii), $W^B=W$. The basis is therefore $dB_t=\eta\,dW_t$ and
\[
dL_t=dF_t+dB_t=(\alpha_t+\eta)\,dW_t \quad\text{on }[0,T].
\]
Put $\alpha_0^+:=a-\eta$, which is positive because $a>\eta$, and $\nu^+:=na/(a-\eta)$, and let the rate--volatility correlation be $\rho^+:=r$, consistent with the requirement $\rho_{B\alpha}=\rho^+$ since $W^B=W$. Evaluating the map \eqref{eq:ident-map} at this parameter, with $\theta=\alpha_0^++\eta=a$:
\[
\sqrt{(\alpha_0^+)^2+2\alpha_0^+\eta+\eta^2}=\alpha_0^++\eta=a,\qquad
\frac{\alpha_0^+\rho^++\eta\rho^+}{a}=\frac{r(\alpha_0^++\eta)}{a}=r,\qquad
\frac{\alpha_0^+\,a\,\nu^+}{a^2}=\frac{\alpha_0^+\nu^+}{a}=n,
\]
the last because $\nu^+=na/\alpha_0^+$ when $\alpha_0^+=a-\eta$. All three entries of the fitted triple are reproduced exactly, not to first order, in the sense fixed before this proof. The forward basis $B_0$ is matched because it is a free initial condition of the model, entering only the strike shift.

For $\mathcal M_-$, set $\rho_{BF}=-1$ and $\rho_{B\alpha}=-r$; condition \eqref{eq:ident-admiss} again holds with equality, and $W^B=-W$, so $dL_t=(\alpha_t-\eta)dW_t$. Put $\alpha_0^-:=a+\eta$, $\rho^-:=r$ and $\nu^-:=na/(a+\eta)$. Then $\theta=\alpha_0^--\eta=a$ and the same three computations give $\sqrt{(\alpha_0^-)^2-2\alpha_0^-\eta+\eta^2}=|\alpha_0^--\eta|=a$, $(\alpha_0^-r-\eta r)/a=r$ and $\alpha_0^-a\nu^-/a^2=n$. Note that $\rho_{B\alpha}=-r$ is the correlation of $W^B=-W$ with $Z$, which is $-\rho^-=-r$ as required.

The intermediate values of $w$ are covered by an explicit one-parameter family joining $\mathcal M_-$ to $\mathcal M_+$. For $p\in[-1,1]$ set $\rho_{BF}:=p$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ident-homotopy}
\theta(p):=\sqrt{a^2-\eta^2(1-p^2)},\qquad
\alpha_0(p):=\theta(p)-p\eta,\qquad
\rho(p):=\frac{ra}{\theta(p)},\qquad
\rho_{B\alpha}(p):=p\,\rho(p),\qquad
\nu(p):=\frac{n\,a^2}{\alpha_0(p)\,\theta(p)} .
\end{equation}
Four verifications, each immediate, show that every member is a model and that the family sweeps the interval.

\emph{It solves the inverse map.} The first two formulas of \eqref{eq:ident-homotopy} are those of \eqref{eq:ident-inverse}, and the last is the third formula of \eqref{eq:ident-inverse}. Only the correlation has to be checked, and it is self-consistent by construction rather than by a fixed-point argument: substituting $\rho_{B\alpha}=p\rho$ into the last equation of \eqref{eq:ident-inverse} gives $\rho\,\alpha_0(p)=ra-\eta p\rho$, that is $\rho\,(\alpha_0(p)+p\eta)=\rho\,\theta(p)=ra$, which is the third formula of \eqref{eq:ident-homotopy}. By Proposition~\ref{prop:ident-inverse} the parameter therefore reproduces $(a,r,n)$ exactly.

\emph{It is admissible.} With $\rho_{BF}=p$ and $\rho_{B\alpha}=p\rho$ the left-hand side of \eqref{eq:ident-admiss} is
\[
p^2-2\rho\cdot p\cdot p\rho+p^2\rho^2=p^2\big(1-\rho^2\big)\ \le\ 1-\rho^2 ,
\]
with equality if and only if $p^2=1$. Every member of the family is admissible and only the two endpoints lie on the boundary of the admissible set. Note that $\rho$ appears as a coefficient of the quadratic form in \eqref{eq:ident-admiss} and varies along the family, so this verification cannot be replaced by an appeal to convexity of a fixed admissible region.

\emph{Its correlation is a correlation.} Since $\theta(p)\ge\sqrt{a^2-\eta^2}$ for $p\in[-1,1]$, we have $|\rho(p)|\le|r|a/\sqrt{a^2-\eta^2}<1$ by the standing hypothesis $|r|<\sqrt{1-\eta^2/a^2}$. Also $\alpha_0(p)>0$ and $\nu(p)>0$ because $\theta(p)>\eta$ under $a>\eta\sqrt2$.

\emph{It sweeps the interval.} By Proposition~\ref{prop:ident-inverse} the map $p\mapsto\alpha_0(p)$ is continuous and strictly decreasing from $\alpha_0(-1)=a+\eta$ to $\alpha_0(1)=a-\eta$, so for every $w\in[-\eta,\eta]$ there is a unique $p\in[-1,1]$ with $\alpha_0(p)=a-w$.

Two features of \eqref{eq:ident-homotopy} are worth naming. In the Cholesky factor of Lemma~\ref{lem:ident-psd}(ii) the relation $\rho_{B\alpha}=\rho\rho_{BF}$ is exactly $c_2=0$, so along the family the basis loads on the rate's own Brownian motion with weight $p$ and on a component independent of both drivers with weight $c_3=\sqrt{1-p^2}$, and carries no loading at all on the part of the volatility driver orthogonal to the rate; at $p=\pm1$ the independent loading vanishes and the family returns $\mathcal M_\pm$ with $\rho=r$. At $p=0$ it returns $\alpha_0=\sqrt{a^2-\eta^2}$, $\rho_{B\alpha}=0$ and $\rho=ra/\sqrt{a^2-\eta^2}$, which is precisely the model of an analyst who sets both basis correlations to zero (Section~\ref{sec:ident-example}, Step 3). The family is therefore a homotopy from one extreme, through the desk's implicit convention, to the other.

By Lemma~\ref{lem:prelim-tc} and Corollary~\ref{cor:prelim-quoted}, the successor at-the-money volatility of a model with parameters $(\alpha_0,\rho,\nu)$ is $\alpha_0\,c^*(\rho,\nu)\,q$, the bracket being evaluated at that model's own $(\rho,\nu)$; substituting $\alpha_0=\alpha_0(p)=a-w$ gives the stated value. By \eqref{eq:ident-alpharange} the range of $\alpha_0$ is exactly $[a-\eta,a+\eta]$, of half-width $\eta$, and no parameter outside that range reproduces $(a,r,n)$ with basis volatility $\eta$, by the second sentence of Proposition~\ref{prop:ident-inverse}. For \eqref{eq:ident-quotedhw}, evaluate $\alpha_0^{\pm}c^{*\pm}q=q[\alpha_0^{\pm}+(2-3r^2)n^2a^2\tau^*/(24\alpha_0^{\pm})]$ at $\alpha_0^{\pm}=a\mp\eta$ and halve the difference, using $\tfrac12(1/(a-\eta)-1/(a+\eta))=\eta/(a^2-\eta^2)$; dividing by $c^*=1+\tfrac1{24}(2-3r^2)n^2\tau^*$ and expanding gives the second form.

\emph{(ii).} Fix $v\in E_\eta$; by Lemma~\ref{lem:ident-psd}(ii) both $v$ and $-v$ are admissible. Define $\mathcal M_{\pm v}$ by setting $(\rho_{BF},\rho_{B\alpha})=\pm v/\eta$ and solving \eqref{eq:ident-map1} for $(\alpha_0,\rho,\nu)$ so that the observed triple is $(a,r,n)$ in both cases; explicitly, to first order,
\[
\alpha_0^{\pm}=a\mp v_1,\qquad \nu^{\pm}=n\Big(1\pm\frac{v_1}{a}\Big),\qquad \rho^{\pm}=r\mp\frac{v_2-rv_1}{a},
\]
which is \eqref{eq:ident-inverse} expanded to first order. The re-solve is well posed because the Jacobian of the observed level, skew and curvature in $(\alpha_0,\rho,\nu)$ is non-singular for $\nu\neq0$ and $|\rho|\neq\sqrt{2/3}$ (Chapter~\ref{ch:prelim}, after Proposition~\ref{prop:prelim-lsc}), and globally invertible by Proposition~\ref{prop:ident-inverse}. Both models then produce the same observed level, skew and curvature up to $O(\varepsilon^2)$, hence the same JIBAR caplet prices to that order at every strike, since by Step 1 of the route to Theorem~\ref{thm:ident} the observed smile depends on the parameter only through $(a,r,n)$ at this order. They match $B_0$ exactly, as in (i).

Now apply \eqref{eq:ident-decomp} to each. With $\Phi_0$ the same for both, because it is built from the common observed triple,
\[
\sigma^{N,\pm v}_F(K')=\Phi_0(K')\mp v_1\Phi_1(K')\mp v_2\nu\Phi_2(K')+O(\varepsilon^2),
\]
and subtracting gives \eqref{eq:ident-twodiff}. At $K'=F_0$ the functions take the values $\Phi_1(F_0)=qc^*$ and $\Phi_2(F_0)=0$, so the at-the-money difference is $2v_1qc^*$. Differentiating \eqref{eq:ident-phi1} and \eqref{eq:ident-phi2} at $u=0$ gives $\Phi_1'(F_0)=-qc^*\rho\nu/\alpha_0$ and $\Phi_2'(F_0)=qc^*/(2\alpha_0)$, so the difference of skews is
\[
2v_1\Big(-\frac{qc^*\rho\nu}{\alpha_0}\Big)+2v_2\nu\frac{qc^*}{2\alpha_0}=\frac{qc^*\nu}{\alpha_0}\big(v_2-2\rho v_1\big).
\]
The half-widths follow from \eqref{eq:ident-extremes}: $\max_{E_\eta}|v_1|=\eta$ and $\max_{E_\eta}|v_2-2\rho v_1|=\eta$, each attained.

\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Reading the two models]\label{rem:ident-reading-two}
The pair $\mathcal M_\pm$ of part (i) is worth stating in words. In $\mathcal M_+$ the basis moves one-for-one with the compounded rate, so the JIBAR rate has volatility $\alpha_t+\eta$: the observed smile is more volatile than the compounded rate, and the successor at-the-money volatility should be marked $\eta$ \emph{below} the observed level. In $\mathcal M_-$ the basis moves one-for-one against it, the JIBAR rate has volatility $\alpha_t-\eta$, and the successor volatility should be marked $\eta$ \emph{above}. Both models are internally consistent, both are calibrated exactly to the observed JIBAR smile, and no observation in $\Sset$ distinguishes them. The desk's choice between them is a choice of $2\eta$ of at-the-money volatility, made on grounds outside the option market.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Why $\rho_{BF}=\pm1$ is not a pathology]\label{rem:ident-extreme}
The extremes are attained at perfect correlation, which invites the objection that the interval is driven by a corner case. Two replies. First, the interval is a supremum over admissible models and a supremum is attained at a corner by construction; the same objection would dismiss every bound in mathematical finance derived from a correlation constraint. Second, the interval is not a step function of the correlation: by \eqref{eq:ident-alpharange} the diffusion coefficient at the clock is $\alpha_0(p)$ with $\alpha_0'(p)=\eta(\eta p/\theta(p)-1)\approx-\eta$, so a correlation of $0.5$ already costs most of the interval: at the parameters of Section~\ref{sec:ident-example}, $\alpha_0(0.5)=56.4831$ bp, a deviation of $12.219$ bp from $a$, which is three fifths of $\eta$ rather than half. What the corner does is fix the constant, and the constant is $\eta$ exactly.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The count}

Three points about the table.

The $m$ basis points do not help. Each $B_{0,i}$ is an observable, read off the two curves by \eqref{eq:prelim-EB} with no model, and it enters the smile map only through the strike shift $s+B_{0,i}$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:ident}(i). It pins that shift and no smile parameter. Adding expiries adds one observable and one unknown for each of level, skew and curvature; the three basis unknowns are the only ones that do not scale, which is why sharing them across expiries makes the deficit finite.

The deficit is a rank statement, not a counting statement. With the recommended thirteen strikes per expiry \citep[\S7.6, p.~25]{sarb2024conv} there are $13m$ volatility quotes against $3m+3$ unknowns, and a naive count would report the system as over-determined for every $m\ge1$. It is over-determined, and the extra equations are genuinely informative: they test whether the observed smile is a SABR smile at all. What they do not do is separate the SABR triple from the basis contamination, because at the order of the expansion the contamination lies in the span of the derivatives of the SABR smile with respect to $(\alpha_0,\rho,\nu)$. The contamination direction is inside the tangent space of the model family, along which the Jacobian in $(\alpha_0,\rho,\nu)$ is non-singular (Chapter~\ref{ch:prelim}, after Proposition~\ref{prop:prelim-lsc}), so no amount of strike coverage rotates it out. Whether higher-order departures from the SABR family carry information about the basis is item (c) of the gap box after Theorem~\ref{thm:ident}.

At first order only two of the three basis unknowns bite. The parameters $(\eta,\rho_{BF},\rho_{B\alpha})$ enter the $O(\varepsilon)$ terms of \eqref{eq:ident-map1} only through the products $v=(\eta\rho_{BF},\eta\rho_{B\alpha})$, so the first-order experiment can at best learn about a two-dimensional object. The volatility $\eta$ on its own appears first in the term $\eta^2T$ of \eqref{eq:ident-kappa2}, that is at $O(\varepsilon^2)$, and Remark~\ref{rem:ident-checks} shows that a basis independent of both drivers is entirely invisible at $O(\varepsilon)$.

\subsection{The dimension of the consistent set}

\begin{proposition}[Consistent set]\label{prop:ident-consistent}
Fix an observed triple $(a,r,n)$ per expiry and a basis volatility $\eta$ with $a>\eta\sqrt2$.
\begin{enumerate}[nosep,label=(\roman*)]
\item With $\eta$ known and the basis parameters shared across expiries, the set of parameters consistent with $\Sset$ is, at $O(\varepsilon)$, a two-dimensional set diffeomorphic to the ellipse $E_\eta$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:ident-psd}, intersected with the constraints $\alpha_{0,i}>0$ and $|\rho_i|<1$.
\item Its image in the space of successor smiles is a two-parameter family. In the coordinates
\[
\hat A:=\frac{\sigma^N_F(F_0)-\Phi_0(F_0)}{q c^*\eta},\qquad
\hat S:=\frac{\partial_{K'}\sigma^N_F(F_0)-\Phi_0'(F_0)}{qc^*\nu\eta/(2\alpha_0)},
\]
the image is, to first order, the ellipse
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ident-region}
\hat A^2+2\rho\,\hat A\hat S+\hat S^2\ \le\ 1-\rho^2 ,
\end{equation}
whose projections onto each axis are $[-1,1]$.
\item With $\eta$ unknown, the consistent set is three-dimensional and the identified set for the diffusion coefficient at the clock is unbounded above, and bounded below only by $0$; it is bounded above only once $\eta$ is bounded.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(i) By Proposition~\ref{prop:ident-inverse} the parameter is a function of $(\rho_{BF},\rho_{B\alpha})$ alone once $(a,r,n)$ and $\eta$ are fixed, and by Lemma~\ref{lem:ident-psd} the admissible set of $v=\eta(\rho_{BF},\rho_{B\alpha})$ is $E_\eta$, which is two-dimensional with non-empty interior for $\eta>0$ and $|\rho|<1$. The map is smooth and injective on $E_\eta$ because $p\mapsto\alpha_0(p)$ is strictly decreasing and $\rho$ is affine in $\rho_{B\alpha}$.

(ii) By \eqref{eq:ident-decomp} and the values of $\Phi_1,\Phi_2$ and their derivatives at $F_0$ computed in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:ident-two}(ii),
\[
\sigma^N_F(F_0)-\Phi_0(F_0)=-qc^*v_1,\qquad
\partial_{K'}\sigma^N_F(F_0)-\Phi_0'(F_0)=-\frac{qc^*\nu}{2\alpha_0}\big(v_2-2\rho v_1\big),
\]
so $\hat A=-v_1/\eta$ and $\hat S=-(v_2-2\rho v_1)/\eta$. In the coordinates $x=v_1$ and $y=v_2-2\rho v_1$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:ident-psd}(iii) the admissible region is $x^2+2\rho xy+y^2\le\eta^2(1-\rho^2)$, and substituting $x=-\eta\hat A$, $y=-\eta\hat S$, which leaves each term unchanged in sign, gives \eqref{eq:ident-region}. The projections are $[-1,1]$ by \eqref{eq:ident-extremes}.

(iii) Take $p=\rho_{BF}=-1$ and $\rho_{B\alpha}=-\rho$, for which the left-hand side of \eqref{eq:ident-admiss} is $1-2\rho^2+\rho^2=1-\rho^2$, so the triple is admissible with equality for \emph{every} $\eta>0$, with no hypothesis relating $\eta$ to $a$. Then $\theta=a$ and $\alpha_0=a+\eta$ by \eqref{eq:ident-homotopy}, and the map \eqref{eq:ident-map} returns $(a,r,n)$ as in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:ident-two}(i) for $\mathcal M_-$. Letting $\eta\to\infty$ gives consistent models with $\alpha_0=a+\eta$ arbitrarily large, so the identified set for the diffusion coefficient is unbounded above. It is bounded below by $0$, since $\alpha_0>0$ is a standing constraint; the display \eqref{eq:ident-alpharange} is not available for this argument because it is proved under $a>\eta\sqrt2$, and $a-\eta$ is not attainable at all once $\eta>a$. The volatility $\eta$ is constrained by the observables only at $O(\varepsilon^2)$ and, in practice, not at all at quote precision.
\end{proof}

\subsection{What would close the deficit}

Four routes close a deficit of two or three, and each has a cost.

A single successor-rate quote per expiry closes one dimension. One at-the-money compounded caplet price fixes $v_1$ exactly, by \eqref{eq:ident-alpharange}; a second quote away from the money fixes $v_2$. This is the cheap route and it is the reason Chapter~\ref{ch:usd} can test the theory on the USD transition, where such quotes exist from late 2021, and Chapter~\ref{ch:zar} cannot.

An option on the basis itself would pin $\eta$ under the pricing measure. No such instrument is quoted, and none is proposed in the market conventions material.

An assumption closes the deficit by decree. Setting $\rho_{BF}=\rho_{B\alpha}=0$ is the implicit convention of a desk that shifts the strike, applies the decay convention and reports the result. Theorem~\ref{thm:ident-two} prices that decree: it is worth up to $\eta$ of at-the-money volatility and up to $\eta\nu/(2\alpha_0)$ of skew per unit of strike.

Historical basis data estimate the nuisance under the physical measure. A record of $L_t-F_t-s$ gives an estimate of the realised basis volatility and of its realised correlations with the rate and with a proxy for its volatility. That estimate is not the pricing-measure quantity, and the discrepancy is precisely the risk premium that no amount of historical data can observe. What the estimate delivers is an \emph{ambiguity set} rather than a value, and an ambiguity set is what the certificate of Chapter~\ref{ch:certification} takes as input: Theorem~\ref{thm:cov} requires only that the valuation-and-hedging policy be fixed on the fitting sample, not that it be correct. The identifiability deficit of this chapter is therefore not fatal to the programme; it is what makes the programme necessary.

\datanote{The available South African basis record is described in Appendix~\ref{app:data}. Two cautions carry over. The SARB synthetic historical ZARONIA overnight-index-swap curve is constructed by shifting the JIBAR zero curve by a rolling median spread, so its basis is a deterministic function of the JIBAR curve; it may be used for discount factors and forwards but never to estimate $\eta$, $\rho_{BF}$ or $\rho_{B\alpha}$, which would be artefacts of the construction. The one published dynamic study of the spread reports a sample mean of $44.15$ basis points and a sample standard deviation of $17.12$ basis points over 4 January 2016 to 31 August 2024, with official ZARONIA only from November 2022 and a SARB proxy before \citep[Sec.~3, p.~5]{alfeus2026}; those are level statistics under the physical measure, not $\eta$.}

\subsection{The book-level consequence: the intervals add}\label{subsec:ident-book}

Table~\ref{tab:ident-count} makes $(\eta,\rho_{BF},\rho_{B\alpha})$ a single vector shared by every expiry; that assumption is what holds the deficit at three rather than $3m$. It has a consequence for a book that the per-caplet statement does not show, and it is the first question a risk manager asks: because there is one nuisance vector, the sign of the mismarking is the same at every expiry, so the identified intervals of a portfolio add rather than diversify.

\begin{corollary}[Identified intervals add across a book]\label{cor:ident-book}
Consider a book of caplets indexed by $i=1,\dots,m$ on windows $[T_i,T_i+\Delta]$, with observed triples $(a_i,r_i,n_i)$, quoting factors $q_i$, Hagan brackets $c^*_i$ and signed at-the-money vegas $\vartheta_i$, each $\vartheta_i$ being the notional times the accrual factor times the Bachelier vega and positive for a long position. Suppose the basis parameters $(\eta,\rho_{BF},\rho_{B\alpha})$ are shared across expiries. Then, at first order, the at-the-money mismarking of the book is the linear function $-v_1\sum_{i}\vartheta_iq_ic^*_i$ of the single unidentified coordinate $v_1=\eta\rho_{BF}$, and the identified interval for the book's value has half-width
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ident-bookreserve}
\eta\,\Big|\sum_{i=1}^m\vartheta_i\,q_i\,c^*_i\Big| .
\end{equation}
For a book long in every expiry, \eqref{eq:ident-bookreserve} is the \emph{sum} of the per-caplet half-widths $\eta\,\vartheta_iq_ic^*_i$. If instead the basis parameters are per expiry, the coordinates $v_1^{(i)}$ vary independently and the half-width is $\eta\sum_i|\vartheta_i|\,q_ic^*_i$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By Theorem~\ref{thm:ident-two}(ii) and the first display in the proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:ident-consistent}(ii), the at-the-money deviation of expiry $i$ is $-q_ic^*_iv_1$, with the \emph{same} $v_1$ at every $i$ because $\eta$ and $\rho_{BF}$ are shared. Summing with the weights $\vartheta_i$ gives the stated linear function, and maximising a linear function of $v_1$ over the projection of $E_\eta$ onto its first coordinate, which is $[-\eta,\eta]$ by \eqref{eq:ident-extremes}, gives \eqref{eq:ident-bookreserve}. In the per-expiry case the mismarking is $-\sum_i\vartheta_iq_ic^*_iv_1^{(i)}$ over the product of $m$ copies of $[-\eta,\eta]$, and the supremum of a sum over a product set is the sum of the suprema.
\end{proof}

Three readings of \eqref{eq:ident-bookreserve}, in decreasing order of comfort.

A book long in $m$ expiries of equal vega carries $m$ times the single-caplet reserve, not $\sqrt m$ times. Nothing about holding many caplets averages the mismarking away, because there is nothing to average over: one number, $\rho_{BF}$, moves every mark in the same direction.

A book long some expiries and short others is partially immunised, and by exactly the amount the shared-parameter assumption dictates. The reserve is the modulus of the \emph{net} vega-weighted sum \eqref{eq:ident-bookreserve}, which vanishes for a vega-neutral book measured in the weights $q_ic^*_i$. This is the only offset the chapter licenses, and criterion V6 of Section~\ref{sec:ident-validation} is what tests the assumption it rests on: under the per-expiry variant the worst case is $\eta\sum_i|\vartheta_i|q_ic^*_i$ and a long-short book is not immunised at all.

The root-sum-square that a risk system would compute is not available here. Writing the book reserve as $\eta(\sum_i\vartheta_i^2q_i^2c^{*2}_i)^{1/2}$ presupposes a probability distribution on the identified set under which the per-expiry deviations are independent and mean zero. This chapter supplies no such distribution, and an identified set is not a distribution: the interval of Theorem~\ref{thm:ident-two} has no coverage probability, as Section~\ref{sec:ident-validation} states again for the validation. Chapter~\ref{ch:zar} uses \eqref{eq:ident-bookreserve} in the reserve decomposition of the converted book, and uses it in the additive form.

\section{Worked example}\label{sec:ident-example}

\subsection{Parameters}

We take the parameters of the worked example of Section~\ref{subsec:prelim-worked}, so that the successor smile is the one already computed there, and add a basis:
\[
T=1\ \text{year},\quad \Delta=0.25,\quad F_0=7.00\%,\quad \alpha_0=0.006,\quad \beta=0,\quad \nu=0.4,\quad \rho=-0.2,
\]
\[
\eta=0.0020,\quad \rho_{BF}=0.3,\quad \rho_{B\alpha}=-0.2,\quad s=16.19\ \text{bp},\quad B_0=28\ \text{bp}.
\]
All volatilities are normal volatilities and are quoted in basis points; $\alpha_0=60$ bp and $\eta=20$ bp annualised, the latter an illustrative round value inside the bracket $[4,64]$ bp that the only published estimate of the basis dynamics permits (Remark~\ref{rem:ident-epsilon} and the data note there). The basis scale is
\[
\varepsilon=\frac{\eta}{\alpha_0}=\frac{20}{60}=0.33333 .
\]
The correlation triple is admissible: the left-hand side of \eqref{eq:ident-admiss} is $0.09-2(-0.2)(0.3)(-0.2)+0.04=0.13-0.024=0.106$, against a right-hand side of $1-0.04=0.96$. The clock is $\tau^*=T+\Delta/3=1.08333$ and the quoting factor is $q=\sqrt{1.08333/1.25}=0.93095$, both from Section~\ref{subsec:prelim-worked}.

\datanote{The spread $s=16.19$ basis points is the fixed three-month JIBAR credit adjustment spread \citep[p.~1]{bisl2025}; see the data note after Definition~\ref{def:prelim-fallback}. The forward basis $B_0=28$ basis points is illustrative and is not a market quote: it is the difference between the sample mean three-month JIBAR-minus-ZARONIA spread of $44.15$ basis points \citep[Sec.~3, p.~5]{alfeus2026} and the fixed spread, rounded to the nearest basis point. The remaining parameters are those of Section~\ref{subsec:prelim-worked} and are illustrative; the Chapter~\ref{ch:usd} calibrations replace them.}

\subsection{Step 1: the observed JIBAR smile}

Work in basis points, so that $\alpha_0=60$, $\eta=20$ and squares are in bp$^2$. By \eqref{eq:ident-map},
\[
a^2=\alpha_0^2+2\rho_{BF}\alpha_0\eta+\eta^2=3600+2(0.3)(60)(20)+400=3600+720+400=4720,
\]
so $a=\sqrt{4720}=68.702$ bp. The first-order value is $\alpha_0+\rho_{BF}\eta=60+6=66.00$ bp; the exact value exceeds it by $2.70$ bp, which is the $O(\varepsilon^2)$ term $(1-\rho_{BF}^2)\eta^2/(2\alpha_0)=0.91\times400/120=3.03$ bp less a third-order correction. At $\varepsilon=1/3$ the second-order term is already half the first-order term.

Next, with $\theta=\alpha_0+\rho_{BF}\eta=66.00$ bp,
\[
r=\frac{\alpha_0\rho+\eta\rho_{B\alpha}}{a}=\frac{60(-0.2)+20(-0.2)}{68.702}=\frac{-12-4}{68.702}=-0.232889,
\]
\[
n=\frac{\alpha_0\theta\nu}{a^2}=\frac{60\times66\times0.4}{4720}=\frac{1584}{4720}=0.335593 .
\]
The first-order values from \eqref{eq:ident-map1} are $r=-0.2+\tfrac13(-0.2+0.06)=-0.24667$ and $n=0.4(1-0.1)=0.36000$. The observed smile itself, from \eqref{eq:prelim-hagan-N0} at expiry $T=1$ with the correction bracket $c_T=1+\tfrac1{24}(2-3r^2)n^2T=1+\tfrac1{24}(1.83729)(0.112623)=1.008622$, has level $68.702\times1.008622=69.29$ bp and skew $rn\,c_T/2=(-0.078156)(1.008622)/2=-0.039415$ per unit of strike, that is $-3.94$ bp per $100$ bp of strike.

For comparison, a market with no basis and the same true triple would show a JIBAR smile of level $60.75$ bp and skew $-4.05$ bp per $100$ bp. The basis has raised the observed level by $8.54$ bp and flattened the observed skew by $0.11$ bp per $100$ bp.

\subsection{Step 2: the target}

The successor smile is the one computed in Section~\ref{subsec:prelim-worked}: at the clock $\tau^*$ the at-the-money normal volatility is $\alpha_0c^*=60\times1.013578=60.81$ bp, and quoted against $T+\Delta=1.25$ years it is $60.81\times0.93095=56.62$ bp. Its skew, quoted, is $\rho\nu c^*q/2=-0.037744$ per unit of strike, that is $-3.77$ bp per $100$ bp of strike.

\subsection{Step 3: the analyst who ignores the basis}

An analyst who takes $\rho_{BF}=\rho_{B\alpha}=0$ reads the true triple off the observed one by Proposition~\ref{prop:ident-inverse} at $p=0$:
\[
\alpha_0=\sqrt{a^2-\eta^2}=\sqrt{4720-400}=\sqrt{4320}=65.727\ \text{bp},\quad
\rho=\frac{ra}{\alpha_0}=-0.243433,\quad
\nu=\frac{na^2}{\alpha_0^2}=0.335593\times\frac{4720}{4320}=0.366667 .
\]
The correction bracket at the clock is $c=1+\tfrac1{24}(2-3(0.059260))(0.134444)(1.083333)=1.011058$, so the reported successor at-the-money volatility is
\[
65.7267\times1.011058\times0.930949=61.8649\ \text{bp},
\]
that is $61.86$ bp, against the truth of $56.62$ bp: an error of $+5.24$ bp, or $9.3\%$ of the level. The reported skew is $\rho\nu c\,q/2=-0.042006$ per unit of strike, that is $-4.20$ bp per $100$ bp, against the truth of $-3.77$. Every observable is matched: the analyst's fit residual on the JIBAR smile is zero by construction.

\subsection{Step 4: the identified interval}

By Theorem~\ref{thm:ident-two}(i) the successor at-the-money volatility is identified within
\[
[a-\eta,\ a+\eta]=[68.702-20,\ 68.702+20]=[48.70,\ 88.70]\ \text{bp at the clock},
\]
attained by the two explicit models
\[
\mathcal M_+:\ \alpha_0^+=48.70\ \text{bp},\ \rho^+=-0.2329,\ \nu^+=\frac{0.335593\times68.702}{48.702}=0.4734,
\]
\[
\mathcal M_-:\ \alpha_0^-=88.70\ \text{bp},\ \rho^-=-0.2329,\ \nu^-=\frac{0.335593\times68.702}{88.702}=0.2599 .
\]
Quoting each against $T+\Delta$ with its own correction bracket, $c^{*+}=1.018587$ and $c^{*-}=1.005603$, gives successor at-the-money volatilities of $46.1820$ bp and $83.0400$ bp, an interval of half-width $18.4290$ bp. This is the exact figure \eqref{eq:ident-quotedhw}: the two brackets differ because $\nu^+\neq\nu^-$, and the naive product $\eta qc^*=20\times0.93095\times1.009340=18.7929$ bp overstates the quoted half-width by $1.94\%$. The exact statement remains that $\alpha_0$ is identified within $[48.70,88.70]$ bp at the clock, of half-width exactly $20$ bp. The truth, $56.62$ bp, and the naive analyst's answer, $61.86$ bp, both lie inside the quoted interval, as they must.

The interval is $18.43/64.61=29\%$ of its own midpoint of $64.61$ bp, and $18.43/61.86=30\%$ of the naive mark; the interval is not centred on the naive mark, and its midpoint of $64.6110$ bp agrees with $\Phi_0(F_0)=64.5557$ bp to within $0.06$ bp. Expressed in price, the Bachelier at-the-money price is $0.39894\,\sigma\sqrt{t}$ per unit notional and accrual, so the identified interval on the caplet premium runs from $0.39894\times0.004618\times\sqrt{1.25}=0.002060$ to $0.39894\times0.008304\times\sqrt{1.25}=0.003704$, a factor of $1.80$ between the cheapest and dearest admissible mark.

In the units a desk works in, the same width is a reserve. The at-the-money vega is $0.39894\sqrt{T+\Delta}=0.446028$ per unit notional and accrual per unit of normal volatility, that is $R4{,}460$ per basis point of volatility on R100 million of notional at the accrual factor $\Delta=0.25$. The half-width of $18.4290$ bp is therefore
\[
100{,}000{,}000\times0.25\times0.446028\times0.001843=R20{,}550
\]
of undiscounted premium per R100 million of notional on this single caplet: the amount a desk must hold, or disclose, purely because the basis correlation is not identified. By Corollary~\ref{cor:ident-book} a book long twenty such expiries holds twenty times that figure and not $\sqrt{20}$ times it.

\subsection{Step 5: the skew tilt}

By Theorem~\ref{thm:ident-two}(ii) the successor skew is identified, at first order, within a half-width of
\[
q\,c\,\frac{\nu\eta}{2\alpha_0}=0.93095\times1.011058\times\frac{0.366667\times0.0020}{2\times0.0065727}=0.052509\ \text{per unit of strike},
\]
that is $5.25$ bp per $100$ bp of strike, where $(\alpha_0,\nu)$ are taken at the observable-based values of Step 3, since those are what an analyst has. Centred on the naive value of $-4.20$, the identified interval for the successor skew is
\[
[-9.45,\ +1.05]\ \text{bp per }100\text{ bp of strike}.
\]
It contains zero. At these parameters the sign of the successor skew is not identified: there is an admissible model, consistent with every JIBAR quote and with both curves, whose successor smile is flat at the money, and another whose successor smile is more than twice as skewed as the naive mark. The ratio of the half-width to the naive skew is
\[
\frac{q c\,\nu\eta/(2\alpha_0)}{|{\rho\nu c q}/2|}=\frac{\eta}{\alpha_0|\rho|}=\frac{20}{65.73\times0.2434}=1.25,
\]
which shows what drives the failure: a basis volatility that is a third of the rate volatility, against a rate--volatility correlation of only a fifth.

\begin{remark}[The expansion parameter is not small in this market]\label{rem:ident-epsilon}
The value $\varepsilon=1/3$ used here is an illustrative round value and not a stress case, but it is not pinned by any available estimate either, and the arithmetic is worth doing in the text because every quotable number in this chapter is proportional to it. The rate volatility $\alpha_0=60$ bp is a plausible three-month caplet level. For the basis volatility the only published dynamic model of the South African spread is the mean-reverting model of \citet[Sec.~5.1, Table~4]{alfeus2026}, and $\eta$ cannot be read off its reported level statistics directly: the sample standard deviation of $17.12$ bp \citep[Sec.~3, p.~5]{alfeus2026} is a level in basis points, whereas $\eta$ is a diffusion coefficient in basis points per square root of a year. Reading the estimated dynamics as an Ornstein--Uhlenbeck process $dB=-\kappa(B-\bar B)\,dt+\eta\,dW^B$ under the physical measure, the stationary level standard deviation is $\eta/\sqrt{2\kappa}$, so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ident-etabracket}
\eta=17.12\ \text{bp}\times\sqrt{2\hat\kappa},\qquad \hat\kappa=0.028 ,
\end{equation}
and the answer depends entirely on the time unit of $\hat\kappa$, which the source leaves ambiguous. With $\Delta t$ in years, $\eta\approx4$ bp and $\varepsilon\approx0.07$; with $\Delta t$ in days and $252$ business days to the year, $\eta\approx64$ bp and $\varepsilon\approx1.07$. The one available estimate therefore does not pin the expansion parameter to within an order of magnitude, and $\eta=20$ bp sits inside the bracket $[4,64]$ bp as a round illustrative value. That the bracket is this wide is itself an identifiability statement of the same kind as the theorem: the quantity that fixes the width of the interval is the one the market does not quote.

At $\varepsilon=1/3$ the first-order theory is a guide to direction and magnitude but not to sign in every entry: from \eqref{eq:ident-map1}, the first-order prediction for $rn$ is $-0.0907$ against the closed-form value $-0.0782$, so the first-order expansion says the observed skew steepens by $13\%$ while the closed form says it flattens by $2\%$. The three statements that survive exactly are the level $a$ of \eqref{eq:ident-map}, the inverse map \eqref{eq:ident-inverse}, and the at-the-money interval \eqref{eq:ident-alpharange}: all three are exact in $\varepsilon$ at leading order in $\nu$, which is why the headline of the chapter is stated at the money. The skew statements are first order and are labelled as such. Closing item (a) of the gap box after Theorem~\ref{thm:ident} means replacing them by exact statements or by bounds with explicit constants.
\end{remark}

\datanote{The bracket $\eta\in[4,64]$ bp of \eqref{eq:ident-etabracket} is computed from $\hat\kappa=0.028$ and the sample standard deviation $17.12$ bp of \citet[Sec.~3, p.~5 and Sec.~5.1, Table~4]{alfeus2026} under an Ornstein--Uhlenbeck reading of that paper's dynamics, taking $\eta=17.12\sqrt{2\hat\kappa}$ with $\hat\kappa$ in inverse years for the lower end and in inverse days, annualised at $252$ business days, for the upper end. The source states that the economic meaning of $\hat\kappa$ ``depends on the sampling scale used in estimation'' and gives both readings for the implied half-life, so no half-life in days or years is quoted here. Three further cautions: the paper's model is a jump diffusion of CIR type and not an Ornstein--Uhlenbeck process, so \eqref{eq:ident-etabracket} is an order-of-magnitude reading of it and not its estimate; the estimate is under the physical measure while $\eta$ in this chapter is under $\Q^{T+\Delta}$; and the units of the reported parameters are not stated as basis points beside the estimates. Every number in this chapter that is proportional to $\eta$ inherits this bracket, and $\eta=20$ bp is used throughout as an illustrative round value.}

\section{What the validation will test}\label{sec:ident-validation}

The South African market cannot test this chapter: there is no quoted successor smile to compare a prediction against. The USD transition from LIBOR to SOFR can, because the successor smile did become observable there. Chapter~\ref{ch:usd} carries out the test; this section states, in advance of seeing the data, what is to be tested and what counts as passing. Stating the criteria here rather than in Chapter~\ref{ch:usd} is deliberate: a criterion chosen after the result is not a criterion.

\subsection{The design}

The predecessor is three-month USD LIBOR and the successor is compounded SOFR plus the fixed spread; the spread, the fixing date and the cessation calendar are recorded in Chapter~\ref{ch:intro} and the data sources in Appendix~\ref{app:data}. For each observation date $t$ in the deployment window and each expiry in the grid, the procedure is:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep,label=(\arabic*)]
\item fit a normal SABR triple $(a,r,n)$ to the quoted LIBOR caplet smile at expiry $T$;
\item shift the strike by $s+B_{0}(t)$, with $B_0(t)$ read off the LIBOR and SOFR OIS curves;
\item apply the time change of Lemma~\ref{lem:prelim-tc} and quote against $T+\Delta$ by Corollary~\ref{cor:prelim-quoted}, producing the predicted smile $\Phi_0$;
\item estimate $\hat\eta(t)$ from the realised volatility of the LIBOR-minus-compounded-SOFR basis over a trailing window, and form the interval of Theorem~\ref{thm:ident-two};
\item compare with the quoted SOFR caplet smile at the same expiry.
\end{enumerate}

Every criterion below is a statement about the estimate $\hat\eta$ and not about $\eta$, and the sampling error in $\hat\eta$ must be reported beside it. Proposition~\ref{prop:ident-consistent}(iii) has already shown that with $\eta$ unknown the identified set is unbounded above, so the interval is only as meaningful as the bound on $\eta$ that produces it. The transmission is one-for-one and needs no new theory: the half-width \eqref{eq:ident-quotedhw} is linear in $\eta$, so a relative standard error $\mathrm{se}(\hat\eta)/\hat\eta$ becomes the same relative standard error on the half-width. For a trailing window of $N_w$ daily observations of a driftless basis with constant volatility, the realised-volatility estimator has relative standard error approximately $1/\sqrt{2N_w}$, which is $4.5\%$ for a one-year window of $252$ observations, or $0.83$ bp on the $18.43$ bp half-width of Section~\ref{sec:ident-example}. That is small against the $29\%$ width of the interval itself and against the $1.94\%$ bracket correction of \eqref{eq:ident-quotedhw}, and it is reported alongside V1 as a band on the criterion rather than folded into it. What cannot be reported as a standard error is the gap between the physical-measure estimate and the pricing-measure quantity: that is a risk premium on basis volatility, it has no sampling distribution, and no lengthening of the window reduces it.

Two design constraints are not negotiable. The comparison may begin only from the first month in which the vendor marks the SOFR surface as \emph{quoted} rather than \emph{derived}: a derived SOFR surface is the LIBOR surface shifted by the spread, and testing the prediction against it would be circular. And $\hat\eta$ must be estimated from the basis record alone, never from the option surfaces, or the interval is fitted rather than predicted.

\subsection{Acceptance criteria, stated in advance}

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{V\arabic*.},leftmargin=2.2\parindent]
\item \textbf{Containment at the money.} For at least $90\%$ of observation-date and expiry pairs in the deployment window, the quoted successor at-the-money normal volatility lies in the interval $[\Sigma^+,\Sigma^-]$ whose endpoints are the two extreme models of Theorem~\ref{thm:ident-two}(i) carried each through its own Hagan bracket,
\[
\Sigma^{\pm}=(a\mp\hat\eta)\,q\Big[1+\tfrac1{24}(2-3r^2)\Big(\frac{na}{a\mp\hat\eta}\Big)^{2}\tau^*\Big],
\]
and not the symmetric interval $\Phi_0(F_0)\pm\hat\eta qc^*$, which by \eqref{eq:ident-quotedhw} is wider by $\tfrac1{12}(2-3r^2)n^2\tau^*$ in relative terms, about two per cent at the parameters of Section~\ref{sec:ident-example}. The observation dates are the Wednesday marks of each week in the deployment window, or the preceding business day when a Wednesday is a holiday, and the expiry grid is $\{1,2,3,5,7,10\}$ years; both are fixed here so that the denominator of the $90\%$ is not adjustable. This is the primary criterion. A failure rate above $10\%$ falsifies either the model class or the estimate $\hat\eta$, and the two are separated by V5.
\item \textbf{Containment of the skew.} For at least $90\%$ of pairs, on the same observation dates and expiry grid as V1, the quoted successor skew lies within $\hat\eta qc^*\nu/(2\alpha_0)$ of $\Phi_0'(F_0)$, where $(\alpha_0,\nu)$ are the observable-based values obtained by solving \eqref{eq:ident-inverse} at $p=0$, that is $\alpha_0=\sqrt{a^2-\hat\eta^2}$ and $\nu=na^2/\alpha_0^2$, exactly as in Step 3 of Section~\ref{sec:ident-example}. Because the skew statement is first order (Remark~\ref{rem:ident-epsilon}), a failure of V2 with V1 passing is evidence about the remainder, not about the theorem.
\item \textbf{Non-triviality.} The interval must be informative: the median half-width, as a fraction of the at-the-money level, is reported, and the chapter records whether it is small enough to be operationally useful. An interval that contains every plausible mark passes V1 vacuously. The threshold set in advance is that a half-width exceeding $25\%$ of the at-the-money level is recorded as uninformative for that expiry. This threshold is not chosen to be passed: at the South African parameters of Section~\ref{sec:ident-example} the half-width is $18.43$ bp against a midpoint of $64.61$ bp, that is $29\%$, so the chapter's own illustration would be recorded as uninformative by its own criterion. That is the point of stating the criterion; a threshold that the chapter's headline example fails is a threshold that can fail.
\item \textbf{Improvement over the naive mark.} The absolute error of the naive prediction $\Phi_0(F_0)$, which is the market convention of shifting the strike and applying the decay, is compared with the absolute error of the interval midpoint after $v_1$ has been estimated from a single successor quote at the shortest expiry in the grid. The test is the paired sign test of the two absolute errors, one-sided in favour of the second, at the $5\%$ level, computed twice: once over the whole expiry grid $\{1,2,3,5,7,10\}$ years pooled across observation dates, and once over the expiries of three years and longer, which is where the theory predicts the gain. Exact ties in the two absolute errors, to the quoted precision of $0.01$ bp, are discarded and the discarded count is reported. The criterion is passed if the second test rejects.
\item \textbf{Sign and magnitude of the level shift.} The realised deviation $\sigma^{N,\text{quoted}}_F(F_0)-\Phi_0(F_0)$, divided by $-\hat\eta qc^*$, is an estimate of $\rho_{BF}$ implied by the option market. It is compared with the realised correlation of the basis with the forward compounded rate estimated from the trailing window. The statistic is the Pearson correlation of the two series across observation dates at a fixed expiry, tested against zero at the $5\%$ level with the critical value taken from a moving-block bootstrap of the pair, the block length being the $b^*$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:block-b} evaluated at the record length and at $r=2$; the bootstrap is necessary because the implied $\rho_{BF}$ series is a trailing-window construction and is strongly autocorrelated by design. Agreement in sign, and a correlation significantly different from zero by that test, is evidence that the physical-measure estimate transfers to the pricing measure. Disagreement is evidence of a basis risk premium and is reported as such; it is not a failure of the theorem, which is a statement about the pricing measure only.
\item \textbf{Stability.} The estimated $\rho_{BF}$ of V5 is reported as a time series, one series per expiry. Two operational definitions are fixed in advance. \emph{Stable} means an interquartile range of the series below $0.2$ in units of correlation, and a maximum absolute difference between the per-expiry series medians below $0.2$. \emph{Moves with the level} means that the ordinary least squares regression of the implied $\rho_{BF}$ on the contemporaneous level $B_0(t)$ has a slope significantly different from zero at the $5\%$ level, by the same moving-block bootstrap as V5. A stable series that does not move with the level supports the shared-across-expiries assumption of Table~\ref{tab:ident-count} and hence the additive book reserve of Corollary~\ref{cor:ident-book}; a series that moves with the level supports the per-expiry variant, a deficit of $3m$, and the reserve $\eta\sum_i|\vartheta_i|q_ic^*_i$. Both outcomes are reported; neither falsifies the theorem, which is conditional on the assumption.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{What the test cannot show}

The interval of Theorem~\ref{thm:ident-two} is a model-implied identified set, not a confidence interval. It has no coverage probability, and the criteria above are containment criteria, not coverage criteria; nothing in V1 to V6 is a hypothesis test of the SABR model itself. A high containment rate is consistent with the interval being correct and also with it being too wide, which is why V3 is stated. A low containment rate is consistent with the interval being too narrow, with $\hat\eta$ being underestimated, and with the basis having a term structure of volatility that Proposition~\ref{prop:ident-termstructure} shows would narrow the interval by $h(\kappa T)$; the chapter separates these by reporting V1 as a function of expiry.

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:ident-discussion}

\subsection{What has been established}

The chapter has shown that the transition from a JIBAR smile to a compounded-ZARONIA smile is, to first order in the basis scale, a strike shift by the spread plus the forward basis, followed by the time change to the clock $T+\Delta/3$, followed by an unidentified perturbation lying in a two-dimensional family. The two missing directions are the products of the basis volatility with its two correlations, and they act on the smile through one function that is flat at the money and one that vanishes there, which is why the at-the-money volatility and the skew are affected separately and why the curvature brings nothing new.

The consequence at the money is sharp and is not an order statement. The diffusion coefficient of the successor rate at the clock is identified within an interval of half-width exactly equal to the basis volatility, both endpoints being attained by models one can write in a line: the basis moving one-for-one with the rate, or one-for-one against it. The half-width of the \emph{quoted} at-the-money volatility is that number multiplied by the closed-form factor \eqref{eq:ident-quotedhw}, which is $-1.94\%$ away from $\eta qc^*$ at the worked parameters, because the two extreme models carry different vol-of-vol and hence different Hagan brackets. Nothing in the observable set distinguishes them, and no improvement in the calibration or in the strike coverage will. A desk that reports a single successor at-the-money volatility is reporting the midpoint of an interval whose width it can compute and should disclose.

The parameter count places the deficit at three, or two at first order, independently of the number of expiries when the basis parameters are shared, and at $3m$ when they are not. The deficit is a rank deficiency and not a shortage of quotes: adding strikes tests the model and does not narrow the interval.

\subsection{Limitations}

The expansion parameter is not small in this market. At $\varepsilon=1/3$ the second-order terms are of the same order as the first-order terms in the skew entry, and the first-order prediction can have the wrong sign, as Remark~\ref{rem:ident-epsilon} shows explicitly. The at-the-money conclusion is unaffected because it is exact in $\varepsilon$, but every first-order statement in this chapter should be read as an order-of-magnitude statement until item (a) of the gap box after Theorem~\ref{thm:ident} is closed.

The basis has been modelled as a driftless Brownian motion with constant volatility under $\Q^{T+\Delta}$. That it is driftless is forced, since it is a $\Q^{T+\Delta}$-martingale by Proposition~\ref{prop:prelim-B-mart}; that its volatility is constant is an assumption, and Proposition~\ref{prop:ident-termstructure} shows how the at-the-money conclusion is modified when it is not. That it is continuous is also an assumption, and the one published dynamic study of the South African spread models it with jumps on monetary-policy dates \citep[Abstract]{alfeus2026}; a jump component would break the cumulant computation of Lemma~\ref{lem:ident-cumulants} at third order and is not treated here.

The nuisance parameters are objects under the pricing measure, and every available estimate of them is an object under the physical measure. The gap between the two is a risk premium on basis volatility that no historical record can observe, and it is the reason the interval of Theorem~\ref{thm:ident-two} should be treated as an ambiguity set rather than as an estimate with an error bar.

Finally, the chapter is silent on whether the model class is right. The quoting convention in which the market conventions paper is written is normal (Bachelier) implied volatility \citep[\S8, p.~26]{sarb2024conv}, with an optional linear volatility decay and the $\Delta/3$ effective maturity \citep[\S8.3.1--\S8.3.2, pp.~28--29]{sarb2024conv}. The choice of SABR as the smile parameterisation inside that convention is this thesis's and not the market's, and normal SABR is not the only analytic class available for compounded-rate smiles: \citet[Thm.~5.1, p.~7]{turfus2023} price the same instrument in a short-rate model with an effective variance quadratic in moneyness, and \citet[Thms.~4.1--4.2]{willems2020} price it with effective SABR parameters under a non-decaying volatility of volatility. Identifiability is a property of the class as well as of the experiment, and the deficit computed here is the deficit of this class.

\subsection{Connection to the rest of the thesis}

The chapter delivers two things to what follows. To Chapter~\ref{ch:certification} it delivers the reason the certification problem exists: the bank must choose a valuation-and-hedging policy from inside an interval that the option market does not narrow, so the policy is not the true model and cannot be assumed to be. Theorem~\ref{thm:cov} needs only that the policy be fixed, which is exactly what a choice from the interval provides, and the ambiguity set of Section~\ref{sec:ident-count} is what makes the choice a choice. To Chapter~\ref{ch:usd} it delivers the six criteria of Section~\ref{sec:ident-validation}, fixed before the data are seen. To Chapter~\ref{ch:zar} it delivers the decomposition of the converted book's reserve: the part attributable to the identified map, which is a computation, and the part attributable to the width of the interval, which is a reserve.
