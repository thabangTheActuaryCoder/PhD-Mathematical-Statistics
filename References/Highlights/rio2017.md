## Notes for writers (Chapter 4 above all)

1. **Locator to use.** `\citep[Thm.~6.2]{rio2017}` for the Fuk–Nagaev inequality, or `\citep[Thm.~6.2, Ch.~6, pp.~101--111]{rio2017}` where the page range helps a reader without the book. `\citep[Ch.~6, pp.~101--111]{rio2017}` remains correct and is the weaker, always-safe form. The Rosenthal inequality is `\citep[Cor.~6.3]{rio2017}`.
2. **The theorem number is verified from the citing literature, not from the printed page.** Two independent 2025 papers cite "Theorem 6.2" of the **2017 edition** (one applying it to indicators), and three more cite "Theorem 6.2" of the 2000 French original for the same inequality. The `\gap{}` item in §`sec:sharp-lower` that says "no theorem or page number has been checked against the printed text" should now read: the number and chapter are corroborated by five independent citing sources, the **printed hypotheses and constants have still not been read**, and the upper half of `\eqref{eq:sharp-minimax}` therefore still rests on an unwritten proof.
3. **Do not identify Rio's r with the mixing exponent silently.** In every restatement above, r ≥ 1 is a **free parameter of the inequality**, chosen by the user; the mixing exponent is a property of the process. The chapter's form (1+Nε²/(rσ²))^{−r/2} + N^{−r}ε^{−(r+1)} is what the inequality gives **after choosing Rio's free r equal to the mixing exponent r of B_r**, and that choice is not free of charge: α([λ/r]) ≤ C(λ/r)^{−r} contributes a constant of order C r^r, alongside the factor 4 on each term and the fact that the left-hand side is P(max_k |S_k| ≥ **4**λ), not P(|S_N| ≥ λ). If the chapter states the bound with constants, it must state them from the printed book; if it states only the shape, it should say that the constants are absorbed and that r is being chosen, not inherited.
4. **The variance proxy is a summed covariance, not a marginal variance.** In (1.7) V = sup_i(E X_i² + 2Σ_{j>i}|E X_iX_j|) and in arXiv:2502.13529 s_n² = Σ_{i,j}|Cov(X_i,X_j)|. Chapter 4's σ² in (1+Nε²/(rσ²))^{−r/2} must be the same object as Lemma `lem:sharp-var`'s ¼ + 2Cζ(r) bound (a summed-covariance quantity), which it is — so the sub-Gaussian estimate of Remark `rem:sharp-constants` uses the right σ². Say so.
5. **Chapter 7 of the book, "Empirical Distribution Functions" (pp. 113–124), is unexamined and may already contain the two-quantile argument Chapter 4 performs by hand.** Whoever obtains the book should read Chapter 7 before Chapter 4's proof is finalised; it is the most likely place for a prior-art collision.
